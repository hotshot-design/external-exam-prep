## Plot in five beats

<Diagram name="english-arc" caption="Tragic arc" />
1. **Act 1 – Venice.** Iago, passed over for promotion in favour of Cassio, vows revenge on Othello. Roderigo and Iago rouse Brabantio, whose daughter Desdemona has secretly married Othello. The Duke sides with Othello; he is sent to defend Cyprus from the Turks.
2. **Act 2 – Cyprus.** The Turkish fleet is wrecked. Iago gets Cassio drunk; Cassio brawls and is dismissed.
3. **Act 3 – the temptation.** Iago plants doubt about Desdemona and Cassio. The handkerchief is stolen by Emilia and given to Iago. Othello demands "ocular proof".
4. **Act 4 – unravelling.** Othello is driven to jealous rage, strikes Desdemona. Iago stages Cassio's "confession"; Othello resolves to kill her.
5. **Act 5 – catastrophe.** Roderigo is killed; Othello smothers Desdemona; Emilia exposes Iago and is killed by him; Othello learns the truth and kills himself.

## Characters (what they represent)

<Diagram name="english-relationships" caption="Character relationships" />
| Character | Key ideas |
|---|---|
| **Othello** | Outsider-general; noble language collapses into fragmented, animalistic speech as jealousy grows. Tragic hero with hamartia (insecurity/credulity). |
| **Iago** | Master manipulator, motive-hunting; shares soliloquies with the audience (complicity); "honest Iago" irony. |
| **Desdemona** | Agency in choosing Othello, then silenced; loyal to the end ("Nobody; I myself"). |
| **Cassio** | Reputation, class and rank; Iago's rival and tool. |
| **Emilia** | Voice on gender and marriage (Act 4.3); her honesty destroys Iago. |
| **Roderigo** | Gull; shows Iago's contempt and exploitation. |
| **Brabantio** | Venetian racism/patriarchy; "Look to her, Moor". |

## Themes and perspectives
- **Jealousy and manipulation:** the "green-eyed monster"; how doubt becomes "proof".
- **Race and otherness:** Othello as Moor in Venetian society; racist language (Iago: "old black ram"; Roderigo: "thick-lips", 1.1); his internalised insecurity ("Haply, for I am black").
- **Gender and power:** women as possessions/property; the contrast between Desdemona, Emilia, Bianca.
- **Reputation and honesty:** Cassio's lament; irony of "honest Iago".
- **Appearance vs reality:** "I am not what I am"; sight imagery and blindness.
- **Love and violence:** love turned to possession and destruction.
- **Order vs chaos:** Venice (order) vs Cyprus (disorder); "Chaos is come again".

## Context and genre (cover this in every essay)
- **Genre: tragedy.** A noble hero with a *hamartia* (flaw) falls through a mix of character and circumstance; the audience feels pity and fear. Othello's flaws: credulity, insecurity, need for certainty. Look for *reversal* (3.3), *recognition* (5.2) and *catharsis*.
- **Soliloquy and dramatic irony.** The audience knows Iago's plan, so it watches events with knowledge Othello lacks. This shapes the "How is the reader invited to view…" questions.
- **Jacobean attitudes (c. 1603–04).** *Race:* "Moor" meant dark-skinned outsider; stereotypes of lust and savagery appear in Iago, Roderigo and Brabantio's language. *Gender:* women were expected to be chaste, silent and obedient; a husband's honour depended on his wife's fidelity ("cuckold" fear). *Honour and reputation:* public name was social currency.
- **Venice vs Cyprus.** Venice = law, commerce, order; Cyprus = a war outpost, away from society's checks. The move to Cyprus (Act 2) removes restraints and exposes character.
- **Marginalised voices.** Desdemona, Emilia and Bianca are silenced or judged by men. Othello is valued for service but never fully accepted. Ask: whose voice is missing or controlled?

## Aesthetic features and stylistic devices to name
- **Soliloquy / aside** (audience complicity with Iago); **dramatic irony**; **imagery** (animals, disease, light/dark, sight); **motif** (handkerchief, "honest"); **juxtaposition** (Venice/Cyprus; Desdemona/Emilia); **verse vs prose** (Othello's shift from verse to broken prose at 4.1); **repetition**; **foreshadowing**.

## Building the essay (about 120 min)

<Diagram name="english-essay" caption="Essay structure and timing" />

<Diagram name="english-paragraph" caption="Paragraph steps" />
**Planning (15 min):** read the question; underline the key concept words; choose a viewpoint; plan 3 body paragraphs; select 1–2 short quotes per paragraph.

**Structure**
1. **Introduction:** contextualise the question → clear thesis (your informed, critical perspective) → roadmap of 3 ideas.
2. **Body paragraph (×3):**
   - Topic sentence linking directly to the question
   - Evidence (short embedded quote)
   - Analysis of device/technique and **effect on audience**
   - Link to context/values (Jacobean attitudes to race, gender, honour) and to the **thesis**
3. **Conclusion:** restate thesis through synthesis, do not just summarise; end on what the play invites the audience to consider.

**Cohesion tips:** use connectives that develop an argument ("Furthermore," "However," "Consequently") and refer back to question words.

**Language:** formal register, precise literary terminology, present tense, avoid first person unless your teacher approves.
