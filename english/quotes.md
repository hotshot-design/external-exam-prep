# Othello – quote analysis

::: warning Check against your edition
Wording and line numbers differ slightly between editions (Folger, Arden, Penguin). Use the version your class studied, and keep quotes **short and embedded** in your sentences. Analysis below is general; add your teacher's readings.
:::

## How to use a quote (the formula)
**Embed** (6–10 words) → **name the technique** → **explain the effect on the audience** → **connect to a value/context** → **link to the question**.

> Example: *Iago's confession "I am not what I am" (1.1) inverts God's "I am that I am", **positioning the audience as complicit confidants** and exposing a world where **appearance and reality are dangerously divided**.*

## Iago – deception and manipulation

### "I am not what I am." (1.1)
- **Context:** Iago's speech to Roderigo in the opening scene; he explains he serves Othello only for his own ends.
- **Technique:** *inversion of scripture* (God's "I am that I am", Exodus); *paradox*; direct address in a confiding tone.
- **Effect:** announces him as a **Vice figure** from morality plays; the audience is told early, creating **dramatic irony** for the rest of the play.
- **Themes:** appearance vs reality, identity, evil.
- **Use for:** "honesty is a mask", manipulation, audience complicity.

### "The Moor is of a free and open nature, / That thinks men honest that but seem to be so, / And will as tenderly be led by th' nose / As asses are." (1.3)
- **Technique:** *animal imagery* (asses), *dehumanising metaphor*, *soliloquy*.
- **Effect:** shows Iago's contempt and his **diagnosis of Othello's flaw: trusting, credulous nature**. The audience is given the "plan" before it happens.
- **Themes:** manipulation, race and otherness (Othello's "open nature" turned into a weakness), hamartia.
- **Use for:** tragic flaw, how Iago exploits character traits.

### "So will I turn her virtue into pitch, / And out of her own goodness make the net / That shall enmesh them all." (2.3)
- **Technique:** *metaphor* (pitch, net), *irony* (goodness becomes a weapon), *soliloquy*.
- **Effect:** chilling clarity of **intent**; Desdemona's virtue is inverted into evidence of guilt. The audience watches the net form.
- **Themes:** manipulation, innocence vs corruption, gender (a woman's virtue is the instrument of her destruction).
- **Use for:** Desdemona as victim; Iago's method; patriarchal systems that make women's reputation fragile.

### "Men should be what they seem." (3.3)
- **Technique:** *dramatic irony* (Iago says this while he is the opposite); *aphorism*.
- **Effect:** the audience hears the hypocrisy; Othello hears reassurance. **Insinuation by sincerity.**
- **Use for:** appearance vs reality, "honest Iago" motif.

## Othello – race, love, collapse

### "She loved me for the dangers I had pass'd, / And I loved her that she did pity them." (1.3)
- **Technique:** *balance and parallelism* (mirrored clauses) in controlled **blank verse**; the **narrative story-telling** that won Desdemona.
- **Effect:** establishes Othello as an eloquent, dignified outsider; their love is built on stories and **mutual recognition**, which sets up the later reversal.
- **Themes:** love, race and otherness (a Moor valued for his exotic heroism), language as power.
- **Use for:** the contrast between Othello's early eloquence and his later fragmentation.

### "Excellent wretch! Perdition catch my soul / But I do love thee! and when I love thee not, / Chaos is come again." (3.3)
- **Technique:** *dramatic irony* (he foretells his own fall); *hyperbole*; *antithesis* of love/chaos; *foreshadowing*.
- **Effect:** love is the **only thing holding his world in order**. When Iago destroys it, chaos does return.
- **Themes:** order vs chaos, love and violence, tragedy.
- **Use for:** foreshadowing, structure of tragedy, how personal collapse mirrors social disorder.

### "Haply, for I am black, / And have not those soft parts of conversation / That chamberers have..." (3.3)
- **Technique:** *self-deprecation*, *listing of perceived deficits*; the hesitation "Haply".
- **Effect:** reveals Othello's **internalised insecurity**: he adopts Venetian prejudice about himself, making him vulnerable to Iago.
- **Themes:** race, belonging, insecurity as a tragic flaw.
- **Use for:** race as the root of vulnerability; Shakespeare's critique of a society that makes Othello doubt his worth.

### "Farewell the tranquil mind! farewell content! … Othello's occupation's gone!" (3.3)
- **Technique:** *apostrophe* and *anaphora* (farewell…); military *lexis* used to mourn love; *elevated blank verse* with a ritual cadence.
- **Effect:** equates the loss of love with the loss of **identity and status**: his soldierly identity depends on being loved and trusted.
- **Use for:** identity, honour and masculinity, language as emotional barometer.

### "Handkerchief—confessions—handkerchief!" (4.1)
- **Technique:** *fragmented prose* replaces verse; *repetition*; *broken syntax*.
- **Effect:** the **collapse of language mirrors the collapse of mind**; the handkerchief has become a symbol of Desdemona's supposed guilt.
- **Use for:** form and structure (verse to prose), jealousy and mental breakdown, motif of the handkerchief.

### "It is the cause, it is the cause, my soul" (5.2)
- **Technique:** *repetition*, *ritual/sacrificial tone*, *light/dark imagery* in the same speech ("Put out the light, and then put out the light").
- **Effect:** Othello **justifies** killing as justice rather than passion; the audience is horrified by the self-deception.
- **Themes:** love and violence, honour, tragic delusion.
- **Use for:** unreliable self-perception; irony that he sees himself as a moral agent.

### "Then must you speak / Of one that loved not wisely but too well; / Of one not easily jealous, but being wrought / Perplexed in the extreme" (5.2)
- **Technique:** *antithesis* (wisely/too well); *euphemism*; *self-narrating* *dramatic control*.
- **Effect:** Othello tries to **author his own legacy**, softening his guilt. Audience may feel pity and critique.
- **Use for:** tragic recognition (anagnorisis), reputation, how a tragic hero narrates himself.

### "I kissed thee ere I killed thee. No way but this, / Killing myself, to die upon a kiss." (5.2)
- **Technique:** *rhyming couplet* (closure), *paradox* of love and murder, *circular structure* (kiss/kill).
- **Effect:** the tragic conclusion is neatly packaged; the **neat couplet contrasts with the mess of the deaths**, forcing the audience to consider whether love can excuse violence.
- **Use for:** tragedy, love and violence, closure and irony.

## Desdemona and Emilia – gender and power

### "I saw Othello's visage in his mind" (1.3)
- **Technique:** *metaphor*, *personal voice*; she asserts **agency** in public.
- **Effect:** Desdemona **defies patriarchy** by choosing love beyond race and father's authority; the Duke (and audience) accept her voice.
- **Use for:** female agency, race as perception, contrast with her later silence.

### "Nobody; I myself. Farewell. / Commend me to my kind lord." (5.2)
- **Technique:** *understatement*, *dramatic irony* (she protects her murderer), *dying lie*.
- **Effect:** her **loyalty becomes self-erasure**; the audience feels the cost of women's idealised obedience.
- **Use for:** gender, innocence, patriarchy.

### "But I do think it is their husbands' faults / If wives do fall." (Emilia, 4.3)
- **Technique:** *direct argument*, *rhetorical logic*, *plain prose-like diction*.
- **Effect:** a **feminist-leaning critique** within a tragedy; Shakespeare gives her moral clarity while men fail.
- **Use for:** double standards, women's voices, contrast between Emilia and Desdemona.

## Cassio, reputation and the world of the play

### "Reputation, reputation, reputation! O, I have lost my reputation! I have lost the immortal part of myself, and what remains is bestial." (Cassio, 2.3)
- **Technique:** *triple repetition*, *hyperbole*, *prose* (a looser, social register); *animal imagery* ("bestial").
- **Effect:** reputation is a **social currency** and equates with identity; Iago's retort ("Reputation is an idle and most false imposition") shows his cynicism.
- **Use for:** reputation vs reality, honour code, Iago's manipulation.

### "Who steals my purse steals trash… But he that filches from me my good name / Robs me of that which not enriches him / And makes me poor indeed." (Iago, 3.3)
- **Technique:** *antithesis* (purse/name), *paradox*, *ironic sincerity*.
- **Effect:** Iago cunningly **teaches Othello to value reputation** just as he plots to destroy Desdemona's; the audience sees the manipulation behind the wisdom.
- **Use for:** reputation, irony, how Iago weaponises values.

## More quotes for likely prompts

### "Give me the ocular proof." / "Villain, be sure thou prove my love a whore" (3.3)
- **Technique:** *imperative*, *insult*, a demand for evidence that Othello fails to apply to Iago's own claims.
- **Effect:** shows Othello **craving certainty** while accepting a handkerchief as proof; **dramatic irony** since the audience knows the "proof" is staged.
- **Themes:** trust, evidence, jealousy, Othello's flaw.
- **Use for:** handkerchief, jealousy, "flaw vs Iago", appearance and reality.

### "I hate the Moor; / And it is thought abroad that 'twixt my sheets / He's done my office." (1.3)
- **Technique:** *soliloquy*, blunt declarative, reported rumour ("it is thought abroad").
- **Effect:** Iago's motive is **sexual jealousy and suspicion** he admits has no proof, so jealousy is shown as self-feeding even in the villain.
- **Use for:** jealousy, Iago's motives, soliloquies, race ("the Moor").

### "My noble father, / I do perceive here a divided duty." (Desdemona, 1.3)
- **Technique:** *formal address*, *balanced phrasing*, assertive logic in public.
- **Effect:** Desdemona claims **agency** in front of the Senate, shifting loyalty from father to husband.
- **Use for:** gender and power, Desdemona, Brabantio, love, the Venice setting.

### "Put out the light, and then put out the light!" (Othello, 5.2)
- **Technique:** *repetition and wordplay* (candle vs life), *imagery* of light and dark.
- **Effect:** the murder becomes a **ritual of control**, showing the tragic irony of destroying the "light" he loves.
- **Use for:** love and violence, imagery of light/dark, the final scene.

### "Villainy, villainy, villainy!" / "I will not charm my tongue; I am bound to speak." (Emilia, 5.2)
- **Technique:** *triple repetition* (exclamation), defiant *declarative*.
- **Effect:** the moment Emilia **breaks her silence** and becomes the play's truth-teller, in defiance of Iago.
- **Use for:** Emilia, gender and power, truth, reputation.

### "Moor, she was chaste. She loved thee, cruel Moor." (Emilia, 5.2)
- **Technique:** *direct address*, *antithesis* (chaste/cruel), repetition of "Moor".
- **Effect:** restores Desdemona's **reputation** after her death and shows the cost of Othello's certainty.
- **Use for:** reputation, Emilia, Desdemona, race and naming.

### "Demand me nothing. What you know, you know. / From this time forth I never will speak word." (Iago, 5.2)
- **Technique:** *imperative*, *enjambment*, *refusal of explanation*.
- **Effect:** Iago keeps his motives hidden, leaving the audience with an **unresolved question** about the cause of evil.
- **Use for:** Iago, manipulation, motive, closure and tragedy.

## Paragraph model (Othello and race)
> Shakespeare presents Othello's vulnerability as rooted in a society that makes him doubt his worth. His admission "Haply, for I am black" reveals **internalised prejudice**, and the hesitation of "Haply" suggests he is testing a thought that Venice has already planted. The *self-deprecating list* of what he lacks, in contrast with his earlier confident rhetoric before the Senate, shows how Iago's insinuations exploit existing insecurity. The audience, positioned to **sympathise** while recognising Othello's complicity, is invited to question a culture that casts the outsider as always *other*. In this way, the play's tragedy is not only personal flaw but also **social prejudice**.

## Practise
1. Pick 3 quotes from the map. For each, write: technique → effect → value in 4 sentences.
2. Take the prompt *"Othello is destroyed by his own flaws, not by Iago."* Choose 4 quotes and plan a for/against structure.
3. Memorise the **first half** of each key quote (enough to embed) rather than the whole speech.
