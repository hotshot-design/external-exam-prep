# English – what the 2024 and 2025 papers show

::: tip Source
Analysis of the QCAA **2024 and 2025 English external assessment** question books, response books and the 2025 marking guide (`snr_english_24_ea_*`, `snr_english_25_ea_*`, `snr_english_eal_25_ea_mark_guide_pub.pdf` in the [paper files](/english/papers)). ## The paper in numbers
| | |
|---|---|
| Marks | **45** (one extended response). Knowledge application (perspectives and representations, ideas that underpin the text, writer's choices) is marked on scales that run up to 6 |
| Length | **800–1000 words** (this is the instruction in the question book) |
| Time | 15 min planning + 120 min writing |
| Choice | **8 texts, 2 questions per text**: you choose one text and one of (a) or (b) |
| Form | Analytical essay, full sentences, paragraphs |

::: warning Word count
800–1000 words is the stated range. In 120 minutes that is a comfortable pace, so quality and precision matter more than volume. Practise writing about 900 words with 8+ embedded quotes rather than a longer, looser essay.
:::

## Othello prompts, 2024 and 2025
| Year | (a) Concept question | (b) "Significance" question |
|---|---|---|
| 2024 | *How is the reader invited to view the concept of **jealousy** in the play?* | *Analyse the significance of **Desdemona's handkerchief** in the play.* |
| 2025 | *What comment does the play make about the importance of **reputation**?* | *Analyse the significance of **Emilia's character** in the play.* |

### Patterns across all eight texts
- **(a) questions** come in three wordings: *"How is the reader invited to view the concept of X?"*, *"What comment does the text make about X?"* and a **statement + "Discuss"** (e.g. *"The play positions us to feel sympathy for Macbeth. Discuss."*). All ask for a **viewpoint on a concept**.
- **(b) questions** are *"Analyse the significance of …"* a **symbol or object** (2024: handkerchief, dictionary), a **setting** (Norfolk, Kuran House), a **place, event or relationship** (2024: landscape, the supernatural, Lydia running away with Wickham, humans and animals) or a **character** (2025: Emilia, Tommy, Margrét, Etienne, Wickham, Raco). Expect any of these for Othello.
- Questions are **short and concept-driven**. They do not tell you which scenes or quotes to use. That is the "unseen" part.
- Concepts are not repeated between years for the same text, so expect a **new concept** on 27 Oct. Concepts so far for Othello: jealousy, reputation. Still untested (and likely candidates): **race and otherness, deception and appearance vs reality, love and trust, gender and power, honour and revenge, manipulation, justice**.
- Significance questions so far: handkerchief, Emilia. Candidates not yet used: **Iago's soliloquies (as a device), Desdemona, Cassio, Brabantio, Bianca, Cyprus vs Venice, the storm, the "green-eyed monster" imagery, the bed/final scene**.

## What the marking guide rewards (45 marks)
Three criteria, each with a scale from "identifies" up to "authoritative":

**1. Knowledge application**
- **Perspectives and representations**: examines relevant perspectives/representations and gives an **interpretation**. The top levels say *authoritative* and *considered*, the middle levels say *interpretation*, and the lower levels just *summarise* or *identify*.
- **Ideas that underpin the text**: examines how the text is underpinned by **cultural assumptions, attitudes, values or beliefs**. This is the "what does the play assume about race, women, honour?" analysis.
- **The writer's choices**: examines how **stylistic or aesthetic choices shape the text** and interprets them. Naming a device is only level 2, and explaining *how it shapes the text* is level 3+.

**2. Organisation and development**
- **Thesis**: top level is a **discriminating thesis** that responds to the question, **developed across the response**, and gives **clear conclusions based on the arguments**.
- **Evidence**: a *well-considered selection* of text evidence, used **explicitly to strengthen arguments**.
- **Sequencing**: logical order of ideas in and **between** paragraphs.
- **Cohesion**: cohesive devices to **connect, develop, emphasise and transition** between ideas.

**3. Textual features**
- Grammar and sentence structure (a *range* of accurate structures), language choices (vocabulary *with discrimination* and appropriate register for an essay writer), punctuation (*accurate and purposeful*), spelling.

### What this means for your writing
| Marker wording | What to do |
|---|---|
| "responds to the question/task" | Use the **key word of the prompt** in the thesis and in every topic sentence |
| "develops arguments to strengthen the thesis across the response" | Each paragraph must **add a new angle** to one overall argument |
| "explicitly" uses evidence to strengthen arguments | After every quote, say **what it proves** |
| "interpretation" not "summary" | Give *your reading* ("this suggests…", "Shakespeare invites us to…"), not retelling |
| "how stylistic or aesthetic choices shape the text" | Technique → effect on meaning/audience |
| "cultural assumptions, attitudes, values" | One paragraph (or part) on **what Jacobean/Venetian society believed** and how the play endorses or challenges it |
| "authoritative" register | Confident formal voice. Avoid "I think", "kind of", and sweeping "all people" |

## 2020–2022 (and 2023) papers: other texts as Othello practice
**Othello was not set in 2020, 2021, 2022 or 2023** (it first appeared in 2024), so there are only the four real Othello prompts above. The paper format has been the same since 2020: **45 marks, 800–1000 words, 15 min planning + 120 min writing, 8 texts with 2 questions each.** The tragedies are the closest precedent, so use their prompts as Othello-shaped practice:

| Past tragedy prompt | Othello version to practise |
|---|---|
| Hamlet 2022: "Analyse how the **ending** of the play invites the audience to view Hamlet." | How does the ending of the play invite the audience to view Othello? |
| Hamlet 2020 (Ophelia), Macbeth 2023 (Lady Macbeth's character) | Analyse the significance of Desdemona's / Iago's / Cassio's character |
| Macbeth 2021: "…the concept of **masculinity**" | Masculinity and honour in Othello |
| Hamlet 2023: "What point of view about **trust** is communicated?" | What point of view about trust is communicated in Othello? |
| Macbeth 2023: "…about **loyalty**" | Loyalty: Cassio, Emilia and Iago's "honesty" |
| Hamlet 2021: "…the concept of **guilt**" | Guilt and remorse in Othello's final speech |
| Macbeth 2020: "**Insecurity** shapes Macbeth's reign. To what extent do you agree?" | "Insecurity drives Othello's downfall." To what extent do you agree? |
| Hamlet 2021 / Macbeth 2022: **relationship** questions | The Othello–Desdemona or Othello–Iago relationship |
| Hamlet 2022: "…about **fathers**" | Brabantio and fatherhood; marriage and family |
| Macbeth 2022: "the symbol of **blood**" | The symbolism of the green-eyed monster, light and dark, or the storm |

**Question wording over time:** 2020–23 mostly used "What perspective/point of view about X is communicated…?", "Analyse the significance/impact of…" and a quote-based "To what extent do you agree?"; 2024–25 use "How is the reader invited to view…", "What comment does the text make…" and "Discuss". In 2022 five of the eight texts asked how **the ending invites the audience to view the protagonist**, so be ready to discuss Othello's final speech and the closing scene. Common types across texts: character significance, relationships, symbols, a place or event, and a concept.

**Still unasked for Othello (likely candidates):** the ending and Othello as a tragic hero, trust, guilt/remorse, race, appearance versus reality, manipulation, masculinity and honour, love, gender and power, justice and revenge, and the characters Desdemona, Iago, Cassio, Brabantio and Bianca, plus Cyprus versus Venice.

**Marking guide, 2020–25:** the structure has not changed: Knowledge application (18), Organisation and development (16) and Textual features (11) make 45. The one notable wording shift is Criterion 1 level 5, from "a **purposeful** interpretation" (2020–22) to "a **considered** interpretation" (2025); level 6 is "**authoritative**" throughout. Since 2022 the thesis must "**respond to the question/task**", and in 2025 arguments must "**strengthen** the thesis across the response". In spelling, "mostly spelt correctly" allows occasional minor lapses.

## Use the past prompts as practice
Plan each in 15 minutes using the [essay plans](/english/essay-plans) and [quote bank](/english/quotes), then pick one to write in full.

| Practice prompt | Source | Plan angle |
|---|---|---|
| How is the reader invited to view jealousy? | QCAA 2024 | Iago's envy vs Othello's sexual jealousy; "green-eyed monster"; jealousy as self-generating; audience invited to see it as *irrational* but *human* |
| Analyse the significance of the handkerchief | QCAA 2024 | Love token → "ocular proof"; patriarchal ownership of Desdemona; how fragmentation ("Handkerchief—confessions") signals collapse |
| What comment does the play make about the importance of reputation? | QCAA 2025 | Cassio's "Reputation, reputation, reputation!"; Iago's "good name" speech; Othello's final self-image; reputation as social currency vs inner truth |
| Analyse the significance of Emilia's character | QCAA 2025 | Foil to Desdemona; handkerchief theft; the speech on husbands; her death as truth-telling; challenges women's silence |

**New prompts to rehearse** (made up in the same style, not QCAA):
- *What comment does the play make about the way race shapes identity?*
- *How is the reader invited to view the concept of trust in the play?*
- *Analyse the significance of Iago's soliloquies in the play.*
- *"Appearance and reality are constantly at odds in Othello." Discuss.*
- *Analyse the significance of Cyprus in the play.*

## Timing plan for 27 Oct (135 minutes total)
| Minutes | Do |
|---|---|
| 0–15 | **Read both (a) and (b) for Othello.** Choose in the first 3 minutes. Underline the key word and write the thesis. List 3 body-paragraph ideas, each with 2–3 quotes |
| 15–40 | Introduction (thesis + signposts), about 150 words |
| 40–110 | Three or four body paragraphs, about 20 minutes each (about 200 words) |
| 110–125 | Conclusion that synthesises, not summarises |
| 125–135 | Proofread: names, apostrophes, quote accuracy |

(The 15-minute planning period is separate from the 120 minutes of writing time. This is a suggested split, not a QCAA rule.)
