# Othello – sample essay plans

::: warning Use as templates, not scripts
The exam question is **unseen**. These plans show how to turn a prompt into a thesis and three arguments in your 15 minutes of planning. Verify quotes against your edition (see [Quote analysis](/english/quotes)) and swap in your class's readings.
:::

## The 15-minute planning routine
1. **(2 min) Decode the prompt.** Underline the key concept, the verb (*discuss, to what extent, analyse*) and any claim in quotation marks.
2. **(2 min) Decide your stance.** Agree, disagree, or agree with qualification ("largely", "partly"). Pick the one you can support with the best evidence.
3. **(3 min) Write a thesis** that answers the prompt in one or two sentences and names your three arguments.
4. **(6 min) Plan 3 body paragraphs:** argument → quote (technique) → effect → context/value → link back.
5. **(2 min) Conclusion idea:** the "so what", i.e. what the play invites the audience to consider.

**Cohesion:** keep key words from the prompt in every topic sentence.

## Plan 1: Manipulation
**Prompt:** *"Othello is a play about the destructive power of manipulation." To what extent?*

**Thesis:** Manipulation drives the plot, but Shakespeare shows it works only because it exploits pre-existing insecurities in a prejudiced society; the destruction is therefore the product of manipulation **and** the values it feeds on.

| Para | Argument | Evidence and technique | Context / value |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Iago's method** is calculated and adaptable | "I am not what I am" (1.1); "turn her virtue into pitch" (2.3); soliloquies create complicity | Vice figure from morality plays; audience sees the plan |
| 2 | **Language is the weapon**: insinuation, repetition, fragments pass into Othello's own speech | "green-eyed monster" (3.3); Othello's collapse into prose, "Handkerchief—confessions—handkerchief!" (4.1) | Jacobean fear of slander and lost reputation |
| 3 | **It works because of what it exploits**: race, insecurity, gender expectations | "Haply, for I am black" (3.3); "led by th' nose" (1.3) | Venetian racism and patriarchy |

**Conclusion angle:** the audience is warned that deception thrives where **trust is conditional** and outsiders are doubted.

## Plan 2: Flaw vs Iago
**Prompt:** *"Othello is destroyed by his own flaws, not by Iago." Discuss.*

**Thesis:** Iago supplies the poison, but Othello's credulity, insecurity and need for certainty make him a willing drinker; Shakespeare presents tragedy as the meeting of **external malice and internal vulnerability**.

| Para | Argument | Evidence and technique | Context / value |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Othello's flaw is real**: he trusts too fast and abandons Desdemona's word | "Give me a living reason she's disloyal" (3.3); he demands "ocular proof" yet accepts a staged scene (4.1) | Tragic hero's **hamartia** |
| 2 | **Iago engineers and chooses**, so he cannot be ignored | "The Moor is of a free and open nature..." (1.3); "So will I turn her virtue into pitch" (2.3) | Iago as active agent, not passive trigger |
| 3 | **Society shapes the flaw**: Othello's insecurity is learned from Venice | "Haply, for I am black" (3.3); Brabantio, "Look to her, Moor" (1.3) | Racism makes his doubt believable |

**Counter-argument sentence (for a high band):** *"While it is tempting to cast Iago as sole cause, such a reading ignores Othello's agency in the murder."*

**Conclusion angle:** responsibility is **shared**, and the play forces the audience to hold both in view.

## Plan 3: Race and otherness
**Prompt:** *How does Shakespeare use representations of outsiders to explore the values of society?*

**Thesis:** Through Othello, Shakespeare exposes a society that celebrates an outsider's usefulness while denying him belonging, revealing prejudice as a structural value, not merely individual malice.

| Para | Argument | Evidence and technique | Context / value |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Venice values Othello's service, not his equality** | "Keep up your bright swords" (1.2) shows dignity; "an old black ram" (1.1) shows animal imagery | Mercantile Venice; racism in the language of "respectable" men |
| 2 | **Othello's eloquence vs his internalised doubt** | "She loved me for the dangers I had pass'd" (1.3) vs "Haply, for I am black" (3.3) | Outsider tries to assimilate and is undermined |
| 3 | **Verse to prose mirrors the outsider's fall** | 1.3 balanced verse vs 4.1 fragmented prose; the final speech "loved not wisely but too well" | Othello tries to rewrite how Venice will remember him |

**Conclusion angle:** the play invites us to ask whether **the real "monster" is the culture that makes an outsider doubt himself**.

## Plan 4: Gender and power
**Prompt:** *How are women represented in Othello, and what does this reveal about gender and power?*

**Thesis:** Women are represented as both agents and property: Desdemona begins with agency and ends silenced, while Emilia's voice exposes the double standards that make female honesty dangerous.

| Para | Argument | Evidence and technique | Context / value |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Desdemona's early agency** | "I saw Othello's visage in his mind" (1.3) | Defiance of father and convention |
| 2 | **Reduced to a possession and a reputation** | Othello: "she's disloyal"; Iago's "turn her virtue into pitch" (2.3) | Marriage and honour in Jacobean society |
| 3 | **Emilia as moral witness** | "But I do think it is their husbands' faults / If wives do fall" (4.3); her final exposure of Iago | Double standards; speaking truth at personal cost |

**Closing consideration:** "Nobody; I myself" (5.2) shows loyalty taken to **self-erasure**, so the audience is positioned to question the ideal itself.

## Plan 5: Appearance and reality
**Prompt:** *"Honesty is a mask in Othello." Discuss.*

**Thesis:** Honesty in Othello is a **performance** that Iago exploits, yet the play also vindicates true honesty through Emilia and Desdemona, suggesting honesty is endangered rather than empty.

| Para | Argument | Evidence and technique | Context / value |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **"Honest Iago" motif**: repeated epithet, **dramatic irony** | "I am not what I am" (1.1); "Men should be what they seem" (3.3) | Reputation depends on seeming |
| 2 | **Sight and proof are unreliable**: "ocular proof" is staged | The handkerchief; Cassio's "confession" (4.1) | Evidence is manipulated; perception is not truth |
| 3 | **Real honesty survives, at a cost** | Emilia's exposure of Iago (5.2); Desdemona's "A guiltless death I die" | Truth-tellers are punished, but truth prevails |

---

## Plan 6: Love and violence
**Prompt:** *To what extent does Shakespeare present love as a destructive force in Othello?*

**Thesis:** Love is not destructive by nature; it becomes so when it turns into **possession** and when others can weaponise it, a distortion Shakespeare traces through Othello's language.

| Para | Argument | Evidence and technique | Context / value |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Genuine mutual love at the start** | "She loved me for the dangers I had pass'd, / And I loved her that she did pity them" (1.3) | Love crossing race and class |
| 2 | **Love is the structure holding Othello's identity**, so threat to it threatens everything | "Chaos is come again" (3.3); "Othello's occupation's gone!" (3.3) | Honour and masculinity |
| 3 | **Love becomes ritual violence** | "It is the cause" (5.2); "I kissed thee ere I killed thee" | Self-justification, tragic irony |

## Plan 7: Reputation (a "wild card" prompt)
**Prompt:** *Analyse how Shakespeare explores the importance of reputation in Othello.*

**Thesis:** Reputation is a social currency in the play, and Iago turns that dependence into a tool of destruction.

| Para | Argument | Evidence and technique |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Cassio's loss of "the immortal part of myself"** | "Reputation, reputation, reputation!" (2.3) – triple repetition, hyperbole |
| 2 | **Iago's cynical flip**, then his sincere-sounding lecture | "Reputation is an idle and most false imposition" (2.3) vs "Who steals my purse steals trash" (3.3) |
| 3 | **Othello and Desdemona are ruined by rumour**; Othello's last attempt to protect his name | "Speak of me as I am" (5.2); "loved not wisely but too well" |

## Plan 8: Trust (concept question)
**Prompt:** *How is the reader invited to view the concept of trust in the play?*
**Thesis:** Trust is shown as both the foundation of love and the weakness through which destruction enters; the audience is invited to value it and to fear how easily it is exploited.
1. **Trust as strength:** Desdemona's trust, "I saw Othello's visage in his mind" (1.3); Othello's trust in "honest Iago".
2. **Trust as exploitable:** "thinks men honest that but seem to be so" (1.3); soliloquy and dramatic irony.
3. **Trust withdrawn without evidence:** "ocular proof" (3.3); handkerchief; fragmented prose (4.1).
4. **Evaluation:** Emilia's late truth-telling (5.2) restores trust too late.

## Plan 9: Honour and revenge
**Prompt:** *"Othello is driven by honour rather than love." Discuss.*
**Thesis:** Othello's violence is presented as an attempt to protect his honour, but the play invites the reader to see this as self-deception.
1. **Honour as public image:** "Her name… is now begrimed and black / As mine own face" (3.3).
2. **Honour as justification:** "It is the cause" (5.2): murder as ritual, "else she'll betray more men".
3. **Love beneath honour:** "I kissed thee ere I killed thee" (5.2); "loved not wisely but too well".
4. **Context:** Jacobean marital honour; Othello as outsider needing status; Iago's own honour/revenge motive.

## Plan 10: Iago's soliloquies (device question)
**Prompt:** *Analyse the significance of Iago's soliloquies in the play.*
**Thesis:** The soliloquies make the audience complicit, expose the instability of Iago's motives, and create the dramatic irony that drives the tragedy.
1. **Complicity:** direct address and plans ("I am not what I am", 1.1; "I hate the Moor", 1.3).
2. **Motive hunting:** shifting reasons (promotion, rumour of Emilia's infidelity, Cassio) suggest "motiveless malignity".
3. **Dramatic irony:** the audience watches Othello trust "honest Iago".
4. **Structure:** they thin out as Iago's control grows; his silence at the end ("Demand me nothing", 5.2).

## Introduction template
> *[Contextualise the question's concept in Jacobean/Shakespearean terms.]* In *Othello*, Shakespeare **[verb: exposes/explores/critiques]** *[concept]* through *[technique 1]*, *[technique 2]* and *[technique 3]*. Ultimately, *[thesis: your viewpoint, using the prompt's key words]*, inviting audiences to *[position/consideration]*.

## Conclusion template
> Through *[restate three ideas as one synthesis]*, Shakespeare demonstrates that *[final judgement]*. The play therefore **[effect on the audience / relevance to values]**, leaving us to consider *[the lasting question]*.

## Timed practice
1. Plan all seven prompts in **15 minutes each** (use only this page's structure, not the content).
2. Choose two plans and practise them separately. For each, plan for 15 minutes and write for 120 (**800–1000 words**). In the exam you write **one** essay.
3. Mark against the objectives on the [overview](/english/): analysis of perspectives and representations, values and attitudes, stylistic devices, structure, cohesion, language.
