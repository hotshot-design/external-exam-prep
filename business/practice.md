# Business – worked case and practice

::: warning Fictional case
"Harbour Home Hardware" is invented for practice. The exam case will be **unseen**, so focus on the method: pull data from the stimulus, apply a tool, evaluate against criteria, recommend.
:::

## Case stimulus (practice)
Harbour Home Hardware is a family-owned chain of 12 stores. Sales have been flat for three years and net profit fell 18% last year as online competitors and a large national retailer took market share. The business has a loyal customer base and strong local brand but has no online store, an ageing workforce, and manual inventory systems. The owners are considering **transformation**: launching e-commerce, upgrading inventory technology and retraining staff. Some long-serving staff fear redundancy. A consultant has recommended a phased change plan.

---

## Step 1: Describe the situation (Objective 1)
- **Life cycle stage:** post-maturity, aiming for **renewal** rather than steady state or decline.
- **Internal:** loyal customers, strong local brand; no online presence; manual systems; ageing workforce.
- **External operating:** online competitors, large national retailer, changing customer expectations.
- **Macro:** consumer shift to online shopping; technology advances; economic pressure.

## Step 2: Identify drivers of change
Competition (online and national retailer), technology, corporate culture (resistance from long-serving staff), financial performance.

## Step 3: Analytical tools

### SWOT
| Strengths | Weaknesses |
|---|---|
| Loyal customers, strong local brand | No online store, manual inventory, ageing workforce, falling profit |

| Opportunities | Threats |
|---|---|
| Growing online market; technology to cut costs; local delivery and click-and-collect | Online rivals and the national retailer; slow response; staff resistance |

**Interpretation:** weaknesses and threats are linked (no online channel makes competitors' advantage worse); the key strength (local brand) can be used to seize the local-delivery opportunity. Implication: transformation should **build on the brand** while fixing digital and operations gaps.

### Force field analysis
| Driving forces (for change) | Strength | Restraining forces (against) | Strength |
|---|---|---|---|
| Falling profit | 4 | Staff fear of redundancy | 4 |
| Online competition | 5 | Cost of new technology | 3 |
| Customer demand for online | 4 | Lack of digital skills | 3 |
| Consultant's plan | 2 | Loyalty to traditional methods | 2 |
| **Total** | **15** | **Total** | **12** |

**Interpretation:** driving forces outweigh restraining forces (15 to 12), so change is **viable**, but staff fear is the largest restraint; reducing it (communication, retraining, participation) is the priority.

## Step 4: Apply a change theory (Lewin or Kotter)
- **Unfreeze:** explain the profit decline and competitive threat to staff (communicate urgency).
- **Change:** launch the e-commerce site; introduce inventory technology; deliver retraining in stages.
- **Refreeze:** embed new processes; update roles and rewards; review performance.

## Step 5: Strategies by function and resistance
| Function | Strategy | Criteria check |
|---|---|---|
| **Financial** | Fund via a bank loan and reinvested profit; staged budget | Efficiency (controls cost); risk of debt |
| **Human resources** | Retrain existing staff, hire 2 digital specialists, voluntary redundancy for those who prefer | Stakeholder satisfaction (staff); effectiveness |
| **Marketing** | Launch online store with click-and-collect, promote the local brand | Competitiveness |
| **Operations** | Barcode inventory, automated reordering | Efficiency, effectiveness |

**Resistance:** use **communication** and **participation** (staff workshops) first; **negotiation** for voluntary redundancy; avoid **manipulation or threat** (damages trust and morale).

## Step 6: Evaluate with business criteria
| Strategy | Competitiveness | Effectiveness | Efficiency | Stakeholder satisfaction | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Phased e-commerce + retraining | High | High | Medium (upfront cost) | High | **Recommend** |
| Cut costs only (close 4 stores) | Low | Low | High | Low | Reject |
| Do nothing | Low | Low | – | Low | Reject |

## Model business report (exam structure, about 295 words)

::: warning Shorter than the exam range
The exam asks for **400–600 words**. This model is shorter so the structure is easy to see. Add data from the stimulus (with numbers) to each section to reach at least 400 words. See the full-length models in the [2025](/business/solutions-2025) and [2024](/business/solutions-2024) solutions.
:::

> **To:** Owners, Harbour Home Hardware  **From:** Business consultant  **Date:** 3 November
> **Subject:** Transforming Harbour Home Hardware
>
> **1. Business situation.** Harbour Home Hardware is a family-owned chain of 12 stores in the post-maturity stage. Sales have been flat for three years and net profit fell 18% as online competitors and a national retailer took market share. The owners seek renewal.
>
> **2. SWOT.**
> - **Strengths:** loyal customers; strong local brand.
> - **Weaknesses:** no online store; manual inventory; ageing workforce (fear of redundancy).
> - **Opportunities:** growing consumer shift to online shopping; inventory and e-commerce technology.
> - **Threats:** online competitors; national retailer; falling profit (18%).
>
> **3. Relationship and trend.** *Relationship:* the weakness of having no online store means the business cannot capture the opportunity of online shopping, so its loyal customers are exposed to competitors who offer it. *Trend:* sales have been flat for three years while net profit fell 18%, showing that without change the business will continue to lose market share. *Implication:* transformation needs to build an online channel while protecting the loyal customer base.
>
> **4. Evaluation of one strategy: a phased e-commerce launch (change management with Lewin's three stages).**
> - **Competitiveness:** an online store lets the business meet market standards set by online rivals, and the local brand differentiates it. *Judgment: high.*
> - **Stakeholder satisfaction:** staff retraining and participation reduce fear of redundancy, and customers gain convenience. *Judgment: high, provided communication is early.* Upfront costs reduce owner returns in the short term.
>
> **5. Decision and recommendation.** The phased launch should **proceed**, because it addresses the main weakness and meets both criteria. It is recommended that the owners unfreeze by communicating the need for change, launch the store in stages with retraining, and refreeze by embedding new procedures, reviewing progress against sales and profit targets every quarter.

*Note how the report evaluates one strategy against two of the four syllabus criteria. Evaluating more strategies against all four criteria is useful practice, but it doesn't match the exam task.*

## Short-response models (marks-focused)
**Explain Lewin's change model (3 marks):** Lewin's model has three stages. In *unfreeze*, management creates readiness by showing why change is needed. In *change*, new processes are implemented. In *refreeze*, the changes are reinforced so they become the new normal.

**Explain one strategy for overcoming resistance (2 marks):** *Participation* involves employees in planning, which builds ownership and reduces fear, but can slow decision-making.

**Evaluate a management style (4 marks):** A *participative* style gains employee ideas and commitment during change, increasing stakeholder satisfaction; however, it is slower and may be ineffective in a crisis requiring fast decisions, so it is most suitable when time allows.
