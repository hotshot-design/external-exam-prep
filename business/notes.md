## Core idea
A business in the **post-maturity stage** (renewal outcome) transforms through **change management**: planned changes to **financial, human resources, marketing and operations** strategies, led by management using a theory/model, and tested against **business criteria**.

## Business life cycle (context)

<Diagram name="business-life-cycle" caption="Life cycle and post-maturity options" />
Establishment → Growth → Maturity → **Post-maturity**: **Steady state**, **Decline**, or **Renewal** (transformation).

## Drivers of change
Corporate culture · management styles · organisational structures · competition · legislative compliance (also technology, consumer trends, economic conditions).

## Change management theories

<Diagram name="business-kotter" caption="Kotter's eight steps" />
**Lewin (3 stages):** Unfreeze (create need for change, reduce resistance) → Change/Move (implement) → Refreeze (embed, stabilise).

**Kotter (8 steps):**
1. Create urgency
2. Build a guiding coalition
3. Form a strategic vision and initiatives
4. **Communicate the vision**
5. **Empower action** (remove obstacles)
6. **Create quick wins** (recognise and reward short-term successes)
7. Build on the change
8. Make it stick (anchor the change in the culture)

*Steps 4–6 use the wording the 2025 exam marked. Other sources use "enlist a volunteer army / enable action / generate short-term wins", which mean the same thing. Check your class notes for the wording of steps 7 and 8.*

*Link to HR:* both depend on communication, leadership and employee involvement.

## Management styles (continuum)
Autocratic → Persuasive → Consultative → Participative → Laissez-faire.
Match style to the situation: crisis may need autocratic; creative change favours participative.

## Strategies for overcoming resistance (syllabus list)

<Diagram name="business-lewin" caption="Lewin's stages" />
Communication · Participation · Negotiation · Manipulation · Threat.
Evaluate each for effectiveness and **stakeholder satisfaction**; manipulation and threat carry ethical and morale risks.

## Performance management
Setting targets, monitoring, feedback and rewards; includes **redundancy, retraining and development** in renewal. Consultants and professional services can supply expertise and objectivity.

## Short-response essentials (2024 and 2025 Section 1)
- **Change management:** a system of ideas and processes for planning, implementing and sustaining change. **Kotter vs Lewin:** Kotter's 8 steps give practical detail and tackle resistance; Lewin's 3 stages (unfreeze, change, refreeze) are simple and give little guidance on each step.
- **Retraining vs development:** *retraining* upskills staff for new priorities or technology; *development* builds longer-term skills and career focus. Both support performance management and reduce resistance.
- **Environmental factors that drive change:** *internal* (organisational structure, corporate culture, management styles), *external operating* (competition, customers, suppliers) and *macro* (economy, technology, law, society). Describe one of each from the case with a stimulus reference.
- **Stakeholders and the power-interest grid:** high power + high interest stakeholders are **managed closely** (e.g. a new partner, dissatisfied members). Analyse their impact on the transformation (positive, negative or neutral).
- **Force field analysis (9 marks in 2025):** two forces *for* change, two *against*, each from the case data with an analysis, plus **one action** to implement change (e.g. a cost-benefit analysis).

## Extra short-response content (from 2020–2022 papers)
- **Lewin refreeze activities:** KPIs and a reward system, a new organisational structure for the new processes, and a feedback system (e.g. customer surveys).
- **Legislative compliance:** new laws (e.g. workplace health and safety) force policy, premises or procedure changes; licensing or qualification rules force HR changes (training, recruitment).
- **Strategic plan objectives:** make them specific, measurable and time-bound, and explain how each achieves the vision for change.
- **Management style as a driver:** autocratic (quick, no staff input) versus democratic or participative (staff views shape the change).
- **Consultants:** used for specialist expertise the business lacks (e.g. market analysis, legal advice).
- **Performance management outcomes:** redundancy, retraining and development.
- **Power-interest grid:** high/high manage closely, high power low interest keep satisfied, low power high interest keep informed, low/low monitor.
- **Evaluating strategies:** judge the strategies the business has used against criteria, then decide and recommend (alternatives belong in the recommendation).

- **Force field analysis:** driving forces vs restraining forces; weigh strength of each; conclude whether transformation should proceed and what to do to tip the balance.

## Functional strategies (evaluate each)
| Function | Examples of transformation strategies |
|---|---|
| **Financial** | Cost control, new capital (equity/debt), budgeting, asset sales, cash-flow improvement, investment in technology |
| **Human resources** | Retraining, redundancy, recruitment of new skills, change leadership, improved engagement, performance management |
| **Marketing** | New products/markets, rebrand, promotion mix, pricing strategy, digital marketing |
| **Operations** | Technology/automation, lean processes, supply chain, quality, sustainability, outsourcing |

## Business criteria for evaluation

<Diagram name="business-criteria" caption="The four business criteria" />
- **Competitiveness** – advantage over rivals
- **Effectiveness** – achieves objectives
- **Efficiency** – use of resources/cost
- **Stakeholder satisfaction** – owners, employees, customers, suppliers, community

## Writing the business report (extended response, 400–600 words)

<Diagram name="business-report" caption="Where the report marks are" />
The 2024 and 2025 reports asked for the same sequence, and the marks sit mainly in steps 3–5 (Synthesising 8 + Evaluating 8 of 24–25):
1. **Heading** (To/From/Date/Subject), a **description** of the business situation with figures (2 marks)
2. **SWOT** with case data in the correct quadrants (4 marks)
3. **One relationship and one trend** from the SWOT, the **effect** of each on the business during transformation, and **conclusions/implications** (8 marks)
4. **Evaluate ONE strategy** (the task names the type: change management in 2025, financial management in 2024) against **TWO of the four business criteria**, with a judgment on each (8 marks)
5. **Decision**, then a **recommendation drawn from the decision**
6. Concise, logical report layout (2–3 marks for Communicating)

*Relationship* = how two factors in the SWOT affect each other (e.g. a weakness limits an opportunity). *Trend* = a direction over time in the data (e.g. falling market share). Time: plan for about 15 minutes, then give the report about **65–70 of the 120 working minutes**, since it carries 24–25 of the 38–41 marks.
