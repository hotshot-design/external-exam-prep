## Section 1: short response (13 marks)

### Q1 (2 marks) Retraining and development
**Marks (guide):** importance of retraining (1) + importance of development (1).
- **Retraining:** staff **upskill in line with the new strategic priorities**, such as new technology or new ways of working, so they can perform the transformed roles.
- **Development:** continuing learning and career programs keep staff skills current and give them a **future focus**, which improves engagement and reduces resistance to the change.

### Q2 (3 marks) Kotter rather than Lewin
**Marks (guide):** explains change management (1) + identifies a reason (1) + explains it (1).
- **Change management:** a system of ideas and processes that supports an organisation to plan, implement and sustain change.
- **Reason:** Kotter's theory gives a **practical eight-step approach** with detail on *how* to lead the change, whereas Lewin's three stages (unfreeze, change, refreeze) are **too simple** and give little guidance on each step. Kotter also focuses on **overcoming resistance** (urgency, coalition, empowering action).

### Q3 (4 marks) Internal and external drivers
**Marks (guide):** identify an internal factor (1) + describe it (1); identify an external operating factor (1) + describe it (1). Use stimulus references.
- **Internal: organisational structure (Stimulus 3).** The structure must change to respond to the declining financial position (fewer roles, a flatter structure, additional managers). It aligns with the plan's "people development" objective (Stimulus 6, 7).
- **External operating: competition (Stimulus 4, 5, 10).** The Gym Business has lost market share since 2019, and reviews say it lacks services and ambience that competitors offer. Industry trends (home gym equipment up 236%, wellbeing programs up, traditional gyms down 15%) show it must evolve.

### Q4 (4 marks) Key stakeholders (Stimulus 9: power-interest grid)
**Marks (guide):** for each of two stakeholders, identify one in the **high power/high interest** box (1) + analyse their impact during transformation (1). The "manage closely" group is the high-power, high-interest one.
- **New partner Anton (positive):** he holds 50% ownership and drives the plan, so the owners must manage his power and interests to keep the transformation aligned with the strategic plan.
- **Current dissatisfied members (negative):** they influence other and potential members through reviews and cancellations, so unresolved dissatisfaction could prevent the business from regaining market share.

Other high-power/high-interest choices from the grid: the two proposed additional managers, potential members.

## Section 2: extended response (25 marks)

### The task (Q5)
Prepare a **business report for the owners on the feasibility of the strategic plan**. Include a description of the business and a **SWOT**. Identify a **trend and a relationship** in the SWOT, interpret their effects and draw conclusions about the implications for the transformation. Use **two business criteria** to evaluate **one financial management strategy** and propose a recommendation about the transformation. (400–600 words.)

### How the guide marks it
| Criterion | Marks | Top-level descriptors |
|---|---|---|
| **Describing** | 2 | *Comprehensive* description of the business situation |
| **Analysing** | 4 | *Significant and relevant* data from the case; each factor correctly classified S/W/O/T; insightful analysis |
| **Synthesising** | 8 | One relevant **relationship and one trend**; *discriminating* interpretation of effects **during transformation**; insightful synthesis of implications; valid conclusions |
| **Evaluating** | 8 | A relevant **financial management strategy from the case**; two business criteria with perceptive evaluations; thoroughly justified decision; appropriate recommendation drawn from it |
| **Communicating** | 3 | Succinct and fluent, and **uses the business report genre purposefully for the audience** |

Compared with 2025, Communicating is worth **3** (report genre matters), and the strategy is a **financial management** strategy.

### Case data to use (stimulus numbers in brackets)

- Gym Business opened 2017 by owners Harper and Hudson; rapid growth to 2019, then closure Apr–Jul 2020 (pandemic), and **profits and membership have declined since 2020**; owners seek a financial injection in 2023 (S2).
- Market share of the business has **declined 2019–2023** (S5). Reviews: limited services, "lacks a vibe", lock-in contracts, but owners are passionate and personal (S4).
- New partner Anton offers **$900 000 for 50%** ownership. The plan expands services (dietitian, physiotherapy, retail), increases online presence and restructures staff (S6, S7).
- **Cost-benefit:** additional costs **$934 470** (private company registration $15 000, wages $342 000, recruitment/training $2 470, refurbishment $500 000, marketing $75 000). Benefits **$1 098 700** (new markets $719 550, retention $379 150). Payback **0.85 years (10.2 months)** (S8).
- **Cash:** investment account $28 529.16 at 4.5% (5-year term to Jan 2026, **$1 500 penalty** for early withdrawal). Transaction account fell from $7 201.67 to **$4 307.08** over Sep–Dec 2023 (withdrawals $32 371.49 exceed deposits $29 476.90). **No budget or forecast** prepared (S6, S8).
- Industry: traditional gyms −15%; wellbeing trends up (60% interested in pilates, 70% self-care) (S10, S11).

Quick checks: $934 470 − $900 000 = **$34 470 shortfall**, and $934 470 ÷ $1 098 700 = 0.85.

### Model report (about 460 words)

> **REPORT**
> **To:** Harper, Hudson and Anton (owners)  **From:** Business consultant  **Date:** January 2024  **Subject:** Feasibility of the 2024–26 strategic plan
>
> **1. Business situation**
> Gym Business opened in 2017 and grew rapidly until 2019. Since the 2020 pandemic closure, profits, membership and market share have declined, and cash is tight. The owners have agreed to a $900 000 investment from Anton for 50% ownership to fund a transformation that adds services, increases online presence and restructures staff.
>
> **2. SWOT analysis**
> | | |
> |---|---|
> | **Strengths** | Passionate, visible owners and a personal feel (S4); loyal foundation members (S2, S4); new investment of $900 000 (S6); a plan with a 10.2-month payback (S8) |
> | **Weaknesses** | Declining profit, membership and market share since 2020 (S2, S5); no budget or forecast (S6); few services and lock-in contracts criticised (S4); falling transaction balance (S8) |
> | **Opportunities** | Growing demand for wellbeing (60% pilates, 70% self-care) and virtual fitness (S10, S11); increased online engagement; expanding services such as dietitian and physiotherapy (S7) |
> | **Threats** | Traditional gyms down 15% (S10); competitors with the "vibe" and no contracts (S4); home gym equipment up 236% (S10); transformation costs above the investment |
>
> **3. Relationship and trend**
> **Relationship:** the customer complaint of limited services (weakness) is linked to the opportunity of wellbeing demand. The business loses members because it doesn't offer what they ask for, so the planned new services directly address the cause of the decline.
> **Trend:** profit and market share have fallen each year since 2020 while demand moves away from traditional gyms (−15%). If nothing changes, losses will continue and the business will run out of cash, so transformation is necessary, not optional.
> **Conclusion:** the plan is aligned to the market, but its financing is the main risk.
>
> **4. Evaluation of a financial management strategy**
> **Strategy:** equity financing, selling 50% of the business to Anton for $900 000 (S6).
> **Criterion 1: efficiency** (use of resources). Total costs are $934 470, so the investment leaves a shortfall of $34 470, and the owners have no budget (S8). The projected benefits ($1 098 700) give a payback of 0.85 years, but those revenues are forecasts for a business with falling sales. Judgment: reasonably efficient, but exposed because the funds do not fully cover the costs.
> **Criterion 2: stakeholder satisfaction** (expectations managed). The owners give up half the ownership and control, so Harper and Hudson's expectations must be managed, but they avoid debt repayments on a weak cash flow, and Anton's investment is welcomed by all parties (S6). Early withdrawal of the investment account would cost $1 500, so the injection is the better option. Judgment: satisfactory if the owners' roles are agreed.
>
> **5. Decision and recommendation**
> **Decision:** the plan is feasible if the equity strategy proceeds, because it funds most of the costs and keeps cash flow available.
> **Recommendation:** accept Anton's investment, prepare a detailed budget and cash flow forecast, find the $34 470 shortfall (for example by staging the refurbishment), and review progress against the strategic plan KPIs every quarter.

### Why this scores
- **Describing:** one tight paragraph with dates and direction.
- **Analysing:** each SWOT item has a stimulus reference and sits in the right quadrant (internal S/W, external O/T).
- **Synthesising:** both a relationship *and* a trend, each with an effect during transformation and an implication.
- **Evaluating:** one financial strategy from the case, two criteria with judgments, a decision, then a recommendation that follows from it.
- **Communicating (3 marks):** a real report layout (To/From/Date/Subject, headings) written for the owners.

::: tip Business criteria (syllabus)
The syllabus lists four business criteria: **competitiveness, effectiveness, efficiency and stakeholder satisfaction**. This model uses efficiency and stakeholder satisfaction. Other financial strategies the case supports: **cash-flow management, budgeting, cost-benefit analysis, debt vs equity financing**.
