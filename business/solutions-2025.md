## Section 1: short response (17 marks)

### Q1 (2 marks) Competition as a driver of change
**Marks (guide):** explains how competition is a driver (1) + a relevant example (1).

*Competition is an **external operating** driver of change. Businesses must monitor competitors and respond quickly to maintain market share and meet customer needs. For example, a coffee shop introduces online ordering because a competitor already offers it and customers now expect it.*

### Q2 (6 marks) Kotter's steps 4, 5 and 6
**Marks (guide):** identify each step (1 each) + explain each (1 each).

| Step | Explanation |
|---|---|
| **4. Communicate the vision** | Share the vision with everyone affected so that it is understood and accepted by all stakeholders |
| **5. Empower action** | Give people the authority and resources to act on the vision and remove obstacles (policies, systems, attitudes) that block the change |
| **6. Create quick wins** | Plan visible short-term successes and recognise and reward staff who achieve them, so the vision is reinforced and momentum builds |

All eight: 1 create urgency, 2 build a guiding coalition, 3 form a strategic vision, 4 communicate the vision, 5 empower action, 6 create quick wins, 7 build on the change, 8 anchor the change in the culture. (Check your class notes for your teacher's wording.)

### Q3 (9 marks) Force field analysis from Case study 1
**Marks (guide):** for each of two forces *for* change and two forces *against* change: identify a relevant force from the stimulus (1) + analyse it (1) = 8, plus **one action** to implement change (1).

**Forces for change**
- **Demand for uploading and creating music:** search data show about 2600 queries a month for "where to upload music demo" and 5400 for "how to make money playing music" (Stimulus 10). Analysis: there is an unmet market, so services such as content creation hubs could reach a broader target market.
- **Market shift to streaming and content creation:** forecast streaming revenue rises towards $4 billion by 2034 while retail purchases decline to under $0.5 billion (Stimulus 9). Analysis: moving into these areas keeps the business aligned with where the market is going.

**Forces against change**
- **Cost:** R&D averages about $1 million and digital change to offer streaming about $1.5 million (Stimulus 7). Analysis: the business needs a timeframe to recoup the outlay before it returns to a steady state.
- **Rising expenses / weak profitability:** expenses have increased over five years (Stimulus 2, 4), which conflicts with the strategic goal of a 20% profit (Stimulus 3). Analysis: limited funds to invest in change.

**Action to implement change:** conduct a **cost-benefit analysis** to find how long it would take to return to profit after the large outlay.

## Section 2: extended response (24 marks)

### The task (Q4)
Prepare a **business report for the owners of Music Co Pty Ltd** to transform their business. Describe the business situation and conduct a **SWOT**. Identify a **relationship** and a **trend**, interpret their effects and draw conclusions about the implications. Using **two business criteria**, **evaluate one change management strategy** to make a decision and propose a recommendation. 400–600 words.

### How the guide marks it
| Criterion | Marks | Top-level descriptors |
|---|---|---|
| **Describing** | 2 | *Comprehensive* description of the business situation |
| **Analysing** | 4 | Relevant data selected; each factor **correctly classified** S, W, O or T; insightful analysis |
| **Synthesising** | 8 | One relevant **relationship** and one **trend** from the analysis; discriminating interpretation of their **effects** on the situation; insightful synthesis of **implications for transformation**; valid conclusions |
| **Evaluating** | 8 | A relevant **change management strategy from the case**; effective use of **two business criteria** with perceptive judgments; a **thoroughly justified decision**; an appropriate **recommendation drawn from the decision** |
| **Communicating** | 2 | Concise, logical sequencing and organisation |

**Mark yourself:** 2 for describing, 4 for SWOT, 8 for relationship/trend, 8 for evaluation, 2 for communication.

### Case data to use (stimulus numbers in brackets)

<Diagram name="sol-2025-swot" />
- Music Co: long-established Queensland music retailer, now a private company, 26% market share, third behind Competitors A and B (30% each) (S7); five regional stores closed in 2024 (S4).
- Revenue, expenses and profit have fallen from their peaks, and expenses have risen in recent years (S2). Strategic plan 2025–2028: access for all generations, in-store competitive advantage and niche, raise revenue to achieve **20% profit** (S3).
- USP scores: Music Co 28, versus competitors 31, 33 and 25. **Brand awareness is Music Co's weakest score (4)** and **industry partnerships its strongest (9)** (S6).
- Options from the consultant: close more stores, keep stores and add **content creation hubs**, move streaming to an app, engage an influencer, sell the business (S5).
- Industry: physical sales are declining, streaming and creation growing (S7, S9); costs of change are large (S7); users search for uploading and creating (S10); heavy music streaming use of 150 minutes per day (S11).

### Model report (about 500 words)

> **REPORT**
> **To:** The owners, Music Co Pty Ltd  **From:** Business consultant  **Date:** 2025  **Subject:** Transforming Music Co
>
> **1. Business situation**
> Music Co is an established Queensland music retailer (long-running, with a timeline from vinyl to streaming, S4) and holds a 26% share of the national market, third behind Competitors A and B. It is in the post-maturity stage: physical music sales are declining, five regional stores closed in 2024, and expenses have risen while profit has fallen. The 2025–2028 strategic plan aims to grow access for all generations, build an in-store niche and reach a 20% profit. Transformation (renewal) is needed to remain viable.
>
> **2. SWOT analysis**
> | | |
> |---|---|
> | **Strengths** | Strongest industry partnerships among the competitors (score 9, S6); established customer base and brand history (S4); existing streaming offer to current customers (S4) |
> | **Weaknesses** | Lowest brand awareness in the market analysis (4, S6); total USP score of 28 trails Competitors 1 and 2 (S6); increasing expenses and falling profit (S2) |
> | **Opportunities** | Streaming and creation are forecast to grow (S9); 2600 monthly searches for uploading demos (S10); content creation hubs growing (S8); high digital engagement (S11) |
> | **Threats** | Physical sales and retail revenue are declining (S7, S9); larger competitors with higher scores (S6); high cost of R&D and digital change (S7) |
>
> **3. Relationship and trend**
> **Relationship:** Music Co's weakness of low brand awareness (4) is linked to the opportunity of online engagement. The business cannot capture growing online demand if customers do not know it. This lowers its ability to compete with Competitors 1 and 2, so any new product must be backed by marketing that builds awareness.
> **Trend:** retail purchases are forecast to fall while streaming and creation revenue rises (S9). The effect is that Music Co's current physical-store model will earn less each year, so staying unchanged threatens the 20% profit goal.
> **Conclusion and implications:** transformation should shift towards digital and creation services while using strong partnerships and keeping costs controlled.
>
> **4. Evaluation of a change management strategy**
> **Strategy:** keep current stores and add content creation hubs that creators can hire (S5).
> **Criterion 1: effectiveness** (the degree to which goals are achieved). Hubs create an in-store competitive advantage and niche and bring in new generations, which supports strategic goals 1 and 2. Demand is evident in searches for uploading music (S10). Judgment: highly effective for goals 1 and 2, with less direct impact on the 20% profit goal.
> **Criterion 2: efficiency** (optimal use of resources). Costs of R&D (about $1 million) and implementation are large, and profit is currently weak (S2, S7). Hubs reuse existing store space, which uses resources better than a full streaming build ($1.5 million), but payback may take years. Judgment: moderately efficient if staged.
>
> **5. Decision and recommendation**
> **Decision:** proceed with the hubs strategy, because it is effective for the strategic goals and more efficient than a new streaming platform.
> **Recommendation:** pilot hubs in two metropolitan stores for six months, use Kotter's steps to communicate the vision and create quick wins, run a social campaign to lift brand awareness, and extend only if bookings reach a target set from a cost-benefit analysis.

Word count: about 500, in the 400–600 range.

### Why this scores
- The SWOT uses **data with stimulus numbers** and each item sits in the right quadrant (Analysing).
- The **relationship** links a weakness to an opportunity and the **trend** is a direction over time; each has an **effect** and an **implication** (Synthesising: 8).
- The evaluation names **one strategy from the case**, applies **two criteria** with a judgment each, and ends with a **decision then a recommendation drawn from it** (Evaluating: 8).
- Headings and short paragraphs earn Communicating.

::: tip Business criteria (syllabus)
The syllabus lists four business criteria: **competitiveness, effectiveness, efficiency and stakeholder satisfaction**. This model uses effectiveness and efficiency. Competitiveness (Music Co's USP score of 28 against 31 and 33) or stakeholder satisfaction would also work.
:::

## Marks lost most often (from the guide's descriptors)
| Slip | Fix |
|---|---|
| Listing SWOT factors without data | Add a number and stimulus reference to each |
| Misclassifying (e.g. a market trend as a strength) | Internal = S/W, external = O/T |
| Giving a relationship **or** a trend | You need **both** for top marks |
| Evaluating several strategies | The task says **one** strategy |
| Decision with no link to the criteria | "Because it meets criterion 1 but only partly meets criterion 2…" |
| Recommendation unrelated to the decision | State a concrete action that follows from it |
