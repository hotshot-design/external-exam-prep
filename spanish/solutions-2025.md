**Paper:** 52 marks. Section 1 (14, Spanish), Section 2 (17, English), Section 3 (21, Spanish email).

## Section 1: audio, answer in Spanish (14 marks)
Each question gets **3 marks for content** (a conclusion plus **two** examples) and **4 marks for the Spanish** (receptive vs productive mode): 4 = few errors, 3 = some errors, 2 = words and isolated phrases, 1 = fragmented meaning.

### Q1 (7 marks): What is the context? Justify with two examples

<Diagram name="sol-es-2025-q1" />
**Guide sample:** *El contexto de estímulo 1 es un discurso en una celebración del cumpleaños de cincuenta años de un padre que da su hijo. Esto es evidente cuando el hablante da la bienvenida a la gente que está allí en esta ocasión especial y pide un aplauso para su padre. También le da las gracias y le desea felicidad en su cumpleaños.*
- **Conclusion (1):** a speech at a father's 50th birthday celebration, given by his son.
- **Example 1 (1):** the speaker welcomes the guests to this special occasion and asks for applause for his father.
- **Example 2 (1):** he thanks his father and wishes him a happy birthday.

**Frame to reuse:** *El contexto es… Esto es evidente cuando…. También…*

### Q2 (7 marks): What does the tone reveal about the speaker's opinion? Two examples

<Diagram name="sol-es-2025-q2" />
**Guide sample:** *El tono del hombre … es agradecido, esto es evidente cuando enumera todo lo que su padre le ha dado en la vida, desde inspiración hasta viajes solidarios. Su opinión sobre su padre es que ha sido una influencia positiva en su desarrollo personal… Muestra esto cuando refiere a su memorable viaje a Bolivia donde ayudó a la gente… Expresa su aprecio por la experiencia tan hermosa que tuvo.*
- **Conclusion (1):** the tone is **grateful** and shows he sees his father as a positive influence.
- **Example 1 (1):** he lists everything his father gave him, from inspiration to volunteering trips.
- **Example 2 (1):** he recalls the memorable trip to Bolivia, helping people (art workshops, children, animals), and expresses appreciation.

**Tip:** name the tone with a precise adjective (*agradecido, emocionado, orgulloso*), then link each example to it.

## Section 2: written texts, answer in English (17 marks)
Pattern: **1 mark for the conclusion + 1 mark per example** (count the examples asked for).

### Q3 (3 marks): Intended audience of Stimulus 2 (blog "La vida después de la escuela")
**Audience:** school leavers looking for guidance on an online forum about what to do after school.
- *Example 1:* the writer acknowledges that students are probably wondering "¿qué hago y en qué orden?" (what to do and in what order: study, work, gap year, travel).
- *Example 2:* the writer addresses them directly ("ustedes… después de graduarse… se acerca el momento de elegir su propio camino") and shares personal anecdotes (deferring university to work and travel).

### Q4 (6 marks): Two perspectives in Stimulus 2, with two examples each

<Diagram name="sol-es-2025-q4" />
**Perspective 1: embracing change is beneficial (3 marks).**
- Example: the writer changed plans, working and travelling to Mexico instead of going straight to university.
- Example: a rhetorical question, "¿a qué esperan?", challenges readers to stop waiting and start their next adventure.

**Perspective 2: school leavers should keep learning (3 marks).**
- Example: the writer promotes university study and other courses.
- Example: personal challenges such as joining a dance class and a volleyball team, enjoyed even though they won't be careers.

Other valid perspectives from the text: *value independence* (moving out, living with international students, practising Spanish).

### Q5 (4 marks): Purpose of the webpage (Stimulus 3)
**Purpose:** to encourage people to use the agency to find temporary work in Spain.
- It offers logistical support (stable work, reputable companies, **medical insurance**) and says "¿Sin experiencia? No hay problema".
- It presents itself as a **guide** that explains how to find work and the requirements and experience needed.
- It lists the **types of jobs** (hospitality, tourism, sales, agriculture) and the cities.
- It says the agency can make the traveller's dreams happen when they join its network ("Únete a nuestra red").

(3 examples needed, plus the purpose = 4 marks.)

### Q6 (4 marks): How does the writer describe Spain as a destination?
**Conclusion:** Spain is described as an appealing destination.
- *Place:* "ciudades pintorescas y rodeadas de mar" (picturesque cities by the sea).
- *Popularity:* one of the most visited countries in Europe for decades.
- *Positive language:* "ciudades llenas de dinamismo", "paisajes preciosos", and calling Spain "el mejor país del mundo".

## Section 3: email in Spanish, 200–300 words (21 marks)

<Diagram name="spanish-email" />
**Task:** an email to your teacher about how learning Spanish has influenced you: (1) feelings about learning Spanish and whether they have changed, (2) a specific experience related to learning Spanish, (3) hopes and aspirations for using Spanish after school.

### How the guide marks it
| Criterion | Top-level descriptors |
|---|---|
| **Information** | **Addresses and elaborates on all three bullets** (elaborating on only two drops you a level) |
| **Meaning** | An **astute** response relevant to the task: selection, **logical sequencing** and **synthesis** of ideas |
| **Language elements** | **Wide range of vocabulary, grammar and tenses**, purposefully and with a high level of accuracy, and **consistent register** for the context |
| **Textual conventions** | **2 marks: a greeting and a sign-off** (1 mark if you have only one of them) |

### The guide's sample response (Jacob), annotated
The sample is a friendly email (*¡Hola, Profe!*, *Jacob*). What it does, in order:
1. **Greeting and purpose:** *¡Hola, Profe! ¿Cómo está? Le escribo para decirle lo agradecido que estoy…* (greeting + purpose + thanks).
2. **Feelings and change:** admits he didn't take part much in lessons because he hadn't yet seen the value of learning Spanish (a feeling that has changed).
3. **Experience:** the *Latin American Fiesta*, where he danced, sang in Spanish and made friends with the DJ. It then widens to making friends from other countries, watching a film without subtitles and understanding everything (*¡Menos mal!*).
4. **Recent evidence:** hearing Spanish accents in the city, and getting a job at a new Spanish restaurant because he speaks the language (*¡me dieron el trabajo!*).
5. **Aspirations:** *Definitivamente continuaré con mis estudios de español… me gustaría inscribirme en un curso en la universidad… espero que eso abra nuevos horizontes.*
6. **Sign-off:** *Gracias por enseñarme Español porque ha enriquecido mi vida. Jacob*

**What to copy:** a **wide range of tenses** (past imperfect, preterite, present perfect `he notado`, future `continuaré`, conditional `me gustaría`, subjunctive `espero que… abra`), natural linkers (*De hecho*, *sin embargo*, *Últimamente*, *Aunque*), exclamations, and a close that returns to the opening idea of thanks.

## What the 2025 guide rewards
| Wording | What to do |
|---|---|
| "identifies a valid … [audience / purpose / perspective]" | State the answer in one clear sentence |
| "provides a relevant example to justify" | Quote or paraphrase one **specific** detail from the text, and give exactly the number requested |
| "conveys meaning relevant to the question with few errors" (Section 1) | Short, accurate Spanish: stick to sentences you can write correctly |
| "addresses and elaborates on all" (Section 3) | Give each bullet its own developed paragraph |
| "uses a wide range of tenses" | Deliberately include past, present, future and a subjunctive |
| "consistent register" | Don't mix *tú* and *usted* |
