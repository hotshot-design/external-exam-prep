**Paper:** 53 marks. Section 1 (15, Spanish), Section 2 (17, English), Section 3 (21, Spanish email).

## Section 1: voicemail audio, answer in Spanish (15 marks)
Content marks are 3 (Q1) and 4 (Q2), plus **4 marks each for the quality of your Spanish** (4 few errors, 3 some errors, 2 words and phrases, 1 fragmented).

### Q1 (7 marks): Who is Juliana trying to contact? Two examples

<Diagram name="sol-es-2024-q1" />
**Guide sample:** *La audiencia de este mensaje de voz es la persona que hace las reservas para el restaurante. Esto se escucha cuando la chica dice que tiene una reserva para la cena de su graduación y que la reserva está a su nombre.*
- **Conclusion (1):** she is contacting the **restaurant staff who take the reservations** (the voicemail is for them).
- **Example 1 (1):** she says she has a reservation for her **graduation dinner**.
- **Example 2 (1):** she says the reservation is **in her name**.

### Q2 (8 marks): Purpose of Juliana's call? Three examples

<Diagram name="sol-es-2024-q2" />
**Guide sample:** *Juliana quiere cambiar su reserva y deja un mensaje en el buzón de voz del restaurante, explicándoles los cambios que desea. Primero, quiere cambiar el horario de la cena. Además, desea sentarse en el jardín y añadir una silla de niños para su prima que acaba de cumplir un año.*
- **Purpose (1):** to **change her reservation**.
- **Example 1 (1):** change the **time** of the dinner.
- **Example 2 (1):** sit in the **garden**.
- **Example 3 (1):** add a **high chair** for her one-year-old cousin.

**Frame:** *El propósito de la llamada es… Primero…, además…, también…*

## Section 2: written texts, answer in English (17 marks)

### Q3 (4 marks): Context of Stimulus 2 ("Costumbre razonable", about the siesta)
**Context:** a student's first-person reflection on the **supposed origins of the Spanish siesta**, told through the legend of Félix (the guide's wording).
Three examples:
1. Félix, a worker in the countryside in the south of Spain, decided to escape the heat in the middle of the day and rest.
2. The sun shines almost all year in the south of the Iberian Peninsula, so days were long and hard.
3. He hid from the sun in the hottest hours (early afternoon), was found asleep in the shade and returned to work with more energy.

### Q4 (4 marks): How could the custom support students in the final years of school?
**Conclusion:** it supports students by giving them **rest that restores energy, so they study better and feel more prepared for success.**
Three examples:
1. Félix returns to work with more energy after sleeping in the shade, as a student would after a rest.
2. The author compares the long, hard days of the countryside with the years of high school, and says studies sometimes need relaxation and rest.
3. "El cansancio puede robarte de la productividad… pero un pequeño descanso cambia todo eso." The author also says the custom helps them be less anxious and more prepared for success at school.

### Q5 (4 marks): Tone of Stimulus 3 ("Idioma del siglo"), with three language choices
**Guide:** the tone is **optimistic** about the Spanish language. (A **cautiously optimistic** or **balanced** answer could be defended because the text also quotes academics' doubts, but the guide's answer is optimistic.)
Three language choices:
1. "**en auge**" (booming), with numbers and percentages ("más de 95 millones", "un incremento de internautas del 65%") to reinforce growth.
2. **Superlatives and comparatives** such as "más hablado", "más de", "segundo idioma más hablado".
3. **Positive qualifiers** such as "valioso", "futuro prometedor".

### Q6 (5 marks): Why does the author believe Spanish is "el idioma del futuro"? Four examples

<Diagram name="sol-es-2024-q6" />
**Conclusion:** because Spanish is **growing quickly and widely**, with the potential to rank first.
1. It is already the **second most widely spoken language after English**.
2. It is the **third most used on the internet**, with a 65% rise in users (2010–2020).
3. The Cervantes Institute's studies show Spanish **rising as a native language relative to English**, so it could replace English.
4. Demand to learn it is growing (18 million students) and it is valuable in international relations and global politics. The author ends: "un futuro prometedor".

## Section 3: email in Spanish, 200–300 words (21 marks)

<Diagram name="spanish-email" />
**Task:** you and your family have just returned from a graduation trip to Argentina. Write an email to the hotel: (1) **thank the cook** for the service and variety of food, (2) **suggest better activities for young people**, (3) **enquire about your lost phone**.

### How the guide marks it
| Criterion | Top-level descriptors |
|---|---|
| **Information** | **Addresses and elaborates on all three** bullets (two elaborated = lower level) |
| **Meaning** | An **astute** response relevant to the task: selection, logical sequencing and synthesis of ideas |
| **Language elements** | **Wide range of vocabulary, grammar and tenses** with a high level of accuracy, and **consistent register** for the context |
| **Textual conventions** | **2 marks:** a **formal opening and a formal closure** (1 mark for one) |

### Model
**Email B** in the [model responses](/spanish/practice#exam-format-emails-200-300-words-based-on-the-2024-and-2025-section-3-tasks) answers exactly this task. It opens with *Estimados señores*, closes with *Atentamente*, gives each bullet a paragraph, and uses preterite, imperfect, conditional, `si` clauses and a question. Write your own version, then check:
- [ ] Formal opening and closing
- [ ] Bullet 1: thanks, with details of the food (variety, a favourite dish)
- [ ] Bullet 2: concrete suggestions for young people (conditional or *podrían*)
- [ ] Bullet 3: describe the phone and where you lost it, and ask what to do
- [ ] Three or more tenses, one subjunctive
- [ ] 200–300 words, formal register throughout (*usted/ustedes*)

## What the 2024 guide rewards
| Wording | What to do |
|---|---|
| "identifies a valid [context / tone / audience / purpose]" | Say it in one clear phrase |
| "relevant example **of a language choice**" (Q5) | Quote words or devices (numbers, superlatives, adjectives), not just ideas |
| "draws a valid conclusion … provides a [first … fourth] relevant example" | Count examples: Q6 needs **four** |
| "addresses and elaborates on all" | Develop every bullet |
| "formal opening / formal closure" | Don't skip them: easy marks |
