## Paper structure (identical shape in both years)
| Section | Marks | Language of answer | Stimulus | Questions |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Short response** | 15 (2024) / 14 (2025) | **Spanish** | **Audio** (Stimulus 1), replayable as often as needed | 2 questions (7 + 8 in 2024, 7 + 7 in 2025) |
| **2. Short response** | 17 | **English** | **Two written texts** (Stimulus 2 and 3) | 4 questions (3–6 marks each) |
| **3. Extended response** | 21 | **Spanish** | A task: write an **email** | 1 question, **200–300 words** |
| **Total** | **53 (2024) / 52 (2025)** | | | |

You get 5 minutes' perusal and 120 minutes' working time, and the audio can be replayed as often as you like.

## Earlier papers: 2020–2022 (older 2019 syllabus)
The 2020–22 papers had a **different structure** from 2024–25. Do not expect the old section order, but the same skills (context, purpose, audience, tone, perspective, "conclusion + examples") recur.
| Year | Total | Section 1 | Section 2 | Section 3 (21 marks, 200–300 words, Spanish) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2020 | 59 | 22 marks, 4 questions, **English** (audio + written text) | 16 marks, 2 questions, **Spanish** (written text) | **Article** for Year 10 students (school magazine) |
| 2021 | 55 | 20 marks, 4 questions, English (audio + written) | 14 marks, 2 questions, Spanish (written) | **Speech** to the whole school (exchange in Argentina) |
| 2022 | 55 | 18 marks, 4 questions, English (written) | 16 marks, 2 questions, Spanish (**audio**) | **Narrative** from one of two images |
| 2023 | 62 | 23 marks, 3 questions, **Spanish** (audio voicemail, 7 + 7 + 9) | 18 marks, 4 questions, **English** (two written texts, 5 + 4 + 4 + 5) | **Review** of a school-leavers' festival in Spain (for the school's social media) |
| 2024 | 53 | 15 marks, 2 questions, **Spanish** (audio) | 17 marks, 4 questions, **English** (written) | **Email** to a hotel |
| 2025 | 52 | 14 marks, 2 questions, Spanish (audio) | 17 marks, 4 questions, English (written) | **Email** to your teacher |

All years: 5 minutes perusal, 120 minutes working. Text types so far: article (2020), speech (2021), narrative (2022), **review (2023)** and email (2024, 2025), so practise writing in several formats. Graduation and celebration themes appeared in 2023 and 2024. The text type changes every year, so **learn the conventions for each**: article and speech (salutation/statement of purpose and concluding remarks), narrative (title, orientation, rising action, resolution), email (greeting and sign-off, or formal opening and closure). **Writing in English scores 0 for Section 3.**

**Task types 2020–22:** purpose and context with 2 examples; five steps in a process (2020 driver's licence, 2022 an audition); how a writer's emotional state changed (4 examples); obstacles; intended audience and community benefit answered in Spanish; two speakers' views; main issue and who is most affected; the writer's influence on young people; tone and what it reveals about character; the author's motivation; a field where Spanish is an advantage.

**2023 (already the 2024–25 shape):** Section 1 was a driving-school voicemail to Rafael (who she is contacting, why she called, how she feels and how she shows it, with 2–3 examples each); Section 2 had an article on the song *Despacito* and a diary entry from Tenerife (what the two created, extent Spanish became a global language, how the change affects the writer's future, how the change in **tone** supports her argument about success). Section 3 bullets: describe the festival and what you enjoyed most; explain the advantages and disadvantages of celebrating far from home; give advice for next year's group. Conventions: a title and the author's name. The 2023 sample used the pluperfect and imperative advice (*no traigan cosas de valor… ¡Disfruten al máximo!*).

**Section 3 tasks**
- 2020 article: share your plans for after graduation, reflect on how studying Spanish has changed your life, and talk about your goals.
- 2021 speech: reflect on past experiences, give advice on getting involved in school life, and how to keep your social life while studying.
- 2022 narrative: what has happened to the person before, what might happen, and what possibilities exist in the future.

**Themes seen only in 2020–22 (still Unit 4 material):** migration and working abroad, volunteering in Uruguay, youth unemployment and the economy, environmental careers, auditions and the arts, blogging and a career change, driver's licences. Topics the syllabus lists but no 2020–25 paper has centred on: **graduation ceremonies and celebrations**, young people shaping the future through technology or sustainability, and global citizenship.

::: warning Syllabus vs papers
The 2025 syllabus says short answers are "up to 100 words" and extended responses "up to 400 words". The papers themselves set **200–300 words** for Section 3, so follow the paper.

## Question types actually asked
| Section | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|
| 1 (audio, answer in Spanish) | Who is Juliana trying to contact? (2 examples) • What is the purpose of Juliana's call? (3 examples) | What is the **context**? (2 examples) • What does the speaker's **tone** reveal about their opinion? (2 examples) |
| 2 (written, answer in English) | Context of Stimulus 2 (3 examples) • How could the custom support students? • Tone of the author (Stimulus 3) • Why does the author believe Spanish is "el idioma del futuro"? | **Audience** of Stimulus 2 (2 examples) • Two **perspectives** (2 examples) • **Purpose** of the webpage • How does the writer **describe Spain** as a destination? |
| 3 (email, Spanish) | Email to a hotel in Argentina after a graduation trip: thank the chef for the food, suggest better activities for young people, enquire about a lost phone | Email to your teacher about how learning Spanish has influenced you: feelings (and whether they changed), a specific experience, hopes/aspirations for using Spanish after school |

### Themes and text types
- **Audio:** a phone call (2024), and a speech at a parent's 50th birthday (2025).
- **Written:** a school-leavers' blog from Mexico (2025), a custom for final-year students (2024), a Spain work-travel web page (2025), an opinion piece on Spanish as the language of the future (2024).
- **Always the Unit 4 topic:** finishing school, future plans and responsibilities (graduation, work, travel, independence, learning and using Spanish).

## The skill pattern: "identify, then justify with N examples"
Almost every short question asks for a **conclusion** (context, purpose, tone, audience, perspective) plus **a stated number of examples** from the text. The marking guide gives:
- **1 mark** for the valid conclusion
- **1 mark per relevant example** (2 or 3, as asked)
- In Section 1 (Spanish), an additional **language mark** for how well you convey meaning in Spanish (4 = few errors, 3 = some errors, 2 = words and isolated phrases, 1 = fragmented).

So the formula is: **State it → Prove it with exactly N pieces of evidence → Stop.** Over-writing earns no extra marks, and in Section 1 more Spanish gives more chances for errors.

### Sentence frames for Section 1 (Spanish)
| Task | Frame |
|---|---|
| Context | El contexto es… Esto es evidente cuando el hablante… También… |
| Purpose | El propósito de la llamada es… Primero…, también…, finalmente… |
| Tone | El tono del hablante es [agradecido / emocionado / nostálgico / formal / urgente]. Esto es evidente cuando… |
| Opinion | Su opinión sobre … es que … Lo demuestra cuando dice que… |

### Section 2 (English) definitions to know
- **Context:** the situation, who is speaking or writing, and why.
- **Purpose:** what the text is trying to do (persuade, inform, advise, advertise, thank, entertain).
- **Audience:** who it is intended for. Evidence is the *language and content that targets them*, such as "tú/ustedes" address, topics that matter to school leavers, or tips on visas.
- **Tone:** attitude, e.g. enthusiastic, reflective, persuasive, grateful, formal.
- **Perspective:** a viewpoint on the topic. Two perspectives can be **different or contrasting**, e.g. "embrace change" vs "plan carefully".

## Extended response: the email (21 marks)
Three bullets, 200–300 words. The marking guide looks at:
- **Information:** you must *address and elaborate on every bullet*. The top level also needs proficient selection, logical sequencing and synthesis of ideas.
- **Meaning:** a clear, effective email that suits context, audience, purpose and conventions.
- **Language elements:** range and accuracy of **vocabulary**, **grammar** and **tenses**, with consistent register for the context.
- **Textual conventions (2 marks):** a **greeting and a sign-off** (2025) or a **formal opening and closure** (2024). One of them earns 1 mark. Writing in English earns 0.

### Email plan (about 250 words)
1. **Opening** (formal if hotel/company, semi-formal if teacher): *Estimado/a señor/a…* or *Estimada profesora…*, a one-line purpose.
2. **Bullet 1** paragraph (about 70 words), then **Bullet 2** (about 70) and **Bullet 3** (about 70). Use a clear linking sentence between them.
3. **Close**: thanks and an offer to follow up, *Atentamente / Un saludo*, name.

**Include a range of tenses:** preterite and imperfect for the experience, present for feelings, future or conditional for hopes, and **one subjunctive** such as *espero que…* or *cuando termine…*. See the [verbs page](/spanish/verbs).

## Practice plan before 28 Oct
1. **Listening:** play each audio three times (gist → details → tone) and answer the Section 1 questions in Spanish. Check against the marking guide's sample answers.
2. **Reading:** do Section 2 of both years in English with the *N examples* rule.
3. **Writing:** draft three emails in 25 minutes each: a thank-you/complaint to a business, a letter to a friend about the future, and an email to a teacher about your learning.
4. Use the [model responses](/spanish/practice) and [vocabulary bank](/spanish/vocabulary) for phrases.
