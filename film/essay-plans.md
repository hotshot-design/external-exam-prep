# Film – stimulus method and essay plans

::: tip Matches the 2024–25 papers
The exam gives **three unseen clips with a context box**. You choose one question, watch the clip, and write an 800–1000 word essay that takes a **viewpoint on how effectively** the clip uses a key concept, with evidence from the clip and the **contexts of production and use**. These plans are frameworks you fill in with what you see. For the actual 2024–25 questions, see [past questions frameworks](/film/past-questions).
:::

## Responding to the stimulus (20 minutes' planning)
1. **Read the three questions and context boxes** (2 min). Note each key concept: Technologies, Representations, Institutions or Audiences.
2. **Choose** the clip and question where you can both **explain the contexts** (the box gives facts) and **notice the most elements**.
3. **Watch the clip** (twice if the format allows). First: gist. Second: list elements by category (camera, mise-en-scène, sound, editing, graphics, technology).
4. **Underline** the key words of the question: the concept, "how effectively", and whether it asks about the target audience.
5. **Write the viewpoint** in one sentence and list 2–3 **interrelationships** that prove it.
6. **Plan the contexts:** 3 facts about production, 3 about use from the context box, each linked to the concept.

## Essay frame (120 minutes writing, 800–1000 words)
- **Intro (about 100 words):** name the clip, the key concept and your viewpoint.
- **Context of production (about 150 words):** who/why/when/funding/technology/institution, linked to the concept.
- **Context of use (about 150 words):** audience, platform, purpose, outcome, linked to the concept.
- **Analysis ×2–3 (about 150–200 words each):** topic sentence → specific evidence (shot, sound, edit, graphic) → **interrelationship** → meaning → **effectiveness judgment** for this audience.
- **Conclusion (about 100 words):** weigh strengths and limits, restate the verdict.

Use **TEEL+E** inside analysis paragraphs: **T**opic → **E**vidence → **E**xplain (how elements work together) → **L**ink to concept and contexts → **E**valuate effectiveness.

## Plan A: Technologies question
**Prompt style:** *How effectively have technologies been used to [build tension / enhance meaning / …]? Justify by analysing the stimulus and explaining the contexts of production and use.*
**Viewpoint frame:** *The technologies are highly effective in … because … , although …*
| Paragraph | Fill in |
|---|---|
| Context (production) | Which technologies were available or used (e.g. CGI, animation, eye-tracking/AR, sound design)? Who made it and why? |
| Context (use) | Where was it shown or used? What did the platform or audience expect? |
| Analysis 1 | Technology A + a second element → meaning (e.g. sound + editing → tension) |
| Analysis 2 | Technology B + a second element → meaning for the audience |
| Evaluation | Compare with a conventional approach; effectiveness for the target audience; one limit |

## Plan B: Representations question
**Prompt style:** *How effectively have ideas/characters/people been represented …?*
**Viewpoint frame:** *The stimulus represents … as … , which is effective because … for audiences who …*
| Paragraph | Fill in |
|---|---|
| Contexts | Who made it, for whom, what views or values shaped it (funding, publication venue, audience age/class) |
| Analysis 1 | How a character/place/idea is **constructed** (costume, setting, performance, framing, sound) |
| Analysis 2 | What is **emphasised or absent**; stereotypes challenged or reinforced; whose perspective is centred |
| Evaluation | How convincing and meaningful the representation is for the audience and purpose |

## Plan C: Institutions question
**Prompt style:** *How has the [agency/studio/platform] created an effective [campaign/product] …?*
**Viewpoint frame:** *The institution has created an effective … by aligning … with … , shown by …*
| Paragraph | Fill in |
|---|---|
| Contexts | Brand/client, agency, commissioning, funding, platform, aim and how success was measured |
| Analysis 1 | How the **product** matches the **platform and audience** (format, technology, incentive, tone) |
| Analysis 2 | How institutional goals (sales, awareness) shape content, and the evidence of success |
| Evaluation | Why this approach suits teenagers/consumers/businesses, and any constraint or risk |

## Plan D: Audiences question
**Prompt style:** *How effectively does [the text] position the audience to [engage with / respond to] …?*
**Viewpoint frame:** *The text positions the audience to … by … , which is effective because …*
| Paragraph | Fill in |
|---|---|
| Contexts | Target audience (age, interests, B2B vs consumer), where it is viewed, how audiences use it |
| Analysis 1 | Techniques for **identification** or **alignment** (humour, point of view, character, direct address) |
| Analysis 2 | How the text builds a **relationship with the brand or idea** (repetition, narrative, product placement, sound) |
| Evaluation | Which audience groups are positioned well or poorly; evidence of response (views, awards) |

## Sentence starters
| Purpose | Starter |
|---|---|
| Viewpoint | "The [technology/representation/campaign] is highly effective in … because …" |
| Production context | "Produced by [institution] in [year] to …, the text …" |
| Use context | "Published on [platform] for [audience], it …" |
| Interrelationship | "The [element A] works with [element B] to …" |
| Interpretation | "This suggests/positions the audience to … because …" |
| Evaluation | "This is effective for [audience] because …, however it is less successful in …" |

## Practice drill (40 minutes)
1. Pick a 1–3 minute clip (use the links in each paper's reference list, or any short film, advertisement or music video).
2. **Plan in 20 minutes** using the steps above. Write the viewpoint, the production and use facts, and 3 interrelationships.
3. **Write one analysis paragraph** (TEEL+E) in 20 minutes.
4. Check against the marking guide: accurate detail? interrelationships? plausible interpretation? a verdict that answers "how effectively"?

## Common errors to avoid
- Describing the plot instead of analysing technique and effect
- Listing techniques without linking them to meaning or to each other
- Skipping the **contexts of production and use** (6 marks)
- Not taking a clear **viewpoint on effectiveness**
- Using one example for the whole essay (aim for evidence in every paragraph)
- Ignoring the **target audience** when the question asks for it
