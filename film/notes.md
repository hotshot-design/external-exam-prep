# Film, TV & New Media – revision notes

::: tip Built around the real exam
The 2024 and 2025 papers each gave **three unseen stimulus clips** (1–3.5 minutes) with a **context box**. You choose **one** question and write an **800–1000 word analytical essay with a viewpoint**. You do not need to have studied the clip, so the skill is a method you can apply to anything. See [past papers analysis](/film/past-papers) and [past questions frameworks](/film/past-questions).
:::

## The five key concepts (syllabus definitions)

<Diagram name="film-five-concepts" caption="Five key concepts" />
| Concept | Definition (QCAA) | Ask of the clip |
|---|---|---|
| **Technologies** | The tools and processes used to create, distribute and access moving-image media | What technology was used (camera, CGI, animation, AR, sound design, platform)? Why? What does it let the makers do? |
| **Representations** | Constructions of people, places, events, ideas and emotions that create meaning | Who or what is shown, and how? Whose view? What is left out? |
| **Audiences** | Individuals and groups for whom products are made, who make meanings when they use them | Who is the target audience? How does the clip position them? How do they use it? |
| **Institutions** | The organisations and people whose operational processes and practices enable or constrain production and use | Who made, funded, commissioned, distributed or regulated it? How did that shape it? |
| **Languages** | Systems of signs and symbols organised through codes and conventions to create meaning | Which codes and conventions create meaning (camera, colour, sound, editing, genre)? |

Every question in 2024–25 was tied to **one** of Technologies, Representations, Institutions or Audiences. **Languages** (codes and conventions) is the toolkit for your analysis in any of them.

## Contexts of production and use (6 of the 32 marks)

<Diagram name="film-contexts" caption="Production and use contexts" />
- **Production** may include: how, when, where and why it was made; who made it; public or commercial financing; available technologies; how it is distributed; the impact of government regulations.
- **Use** may include: who it is made for; why it is used; how it is shaped for users as consumers, citizens or imaginative beings; who can and can't access it; market research; how audiences make meaning of it or adapt it; classification and regulation; platform and viewing context.
- The **context box** in the exam hands you most of these facts. Use them, **separately**, for production and for use, then link them to the key concept.

## The question template
Almost every question is: *"How effectively [has/does/do] … [concept in the stimulus]? Justify your viewpoint by analysing [and appraising] the stimulus, also considering/explaining the contexts of production and use [including the target audience]."* So you must:
1. **Take a viewpoint** on effectiveness (not just describe).
2. **Analyse** relevant elements of the stimulus and the **relationships between them**.
3. **Explain** production and use contexts.
4. **Support** every judgment with specific evidence from the clip.

## Structure (20 min plan + 120 min writing, 800–1000 words)

<Diagram name="film-essay" caption="Exam timing" />
- **Intro:** the clip, the key concept, your viewpoint in one sentence.
- **Context of production** paragraph, **context of use** paragraph (both linked to the concept).
- **2–3 analysis paragraphs**, each on one relationship between elements, ending with an effectiveness judgment.
- **Conclusion:** your verdict and a final weighing of strengths against limits.

## Analysis toolkit: Languages in action
| Area | Terms |
|---|---|
| **Technical codes** | shot size (ECU–ELS), camera angle, camera movement, framing, composition, depth of field, focus |
| **Symbolic codes** | colour, costume, setting, props, gesture |
| **Audio** | diegetic/non-diegetic, score, sound bridge, silence, foley, sound design |
| **Editing** | continuity, montage, jump cut, match cut, cross-cutting, pace |
| **Mise-en-scène** | setting, lighting, props, costume/make-up, actor placement |
| **Conventions** | genre, narrative structure, character types |
| **Production** | pre-production, production, post-production, VFX, CGI, animation, AR |
| **Concepts** | representation, audience, institution, ideology, intertextuality |

**Interrelationships:** the marking guide rewards identifying how elements work *together* (e.g. low-key lighting + sparse score + slow cutting build dread). Aim for at least two combinations per paragraph.

## Concept-specific angles
- **Technologies:** what the technology enables (tension, immersion, personalisation, realism), conventional vs innovative use, how it fits the platform. *Examples from past papers:* CGI and sound for tension, an AR lens with eye-tracking, music-video effects.
- **Representations:** who is constructed as hero/other, stereotypes challenged or reinforced, values and ideologies, how the representation suits the audience and purpose. *Examples:* characters representing picture and sound, ideas of the future.
- **Institutions:** commissioning, funding, platform rules, brand and campaign aims, how success is measured (views, sales), how the institution constrains or enables content. *Example:* an agency building a campaign for teenagers.
- **Audiences:** target demographic, how the text positions viewers (humour, identification, direct address), how the audience uses the text, preferred vs oppositional readings. *Example:* a B2B web film and brand engagement.

## Appraising: writing the verdict

<Diagram name="film-marking" caption="Where the marks sit" />
- Use **comparative strength**: "highly effective because… though less so for…".
- Support with **pertinent evidence** (specific shot, sound, edit, graphic) after each claim.
- Judge **effectiveness for the purpose and audience** the context box gives you, not your personal taste.

## Sentence stems
- "The [technology] is effective in [communicating X] because … , which positions the audience to …"
- "This is reinforced by the interrelationship between … and …, which together suggest …"
- "Produced by [institution] for [audience], the text uses … to achieve its purpose of …"
- "Although … , the stimulus is ultimately [highly/moderately] effective because …"

## Optional background (use only if relevant)
The syllabus lists film movements and artists as Unit 4 (Artistry) learning (French New Wave, German Expressionism, Italian Neorealism, Soviet Montage, Surrealism; auteurs such as Kurosawa, Miyazaki, Wes Anderson). These can enrich **context** or comparisons ("like jump cuts in the French New Wave…"), but the exam is marked on the **stimulus**, so don't force a studied example in.
