## Method for any stimulus question (140 minutes: 20 planning + 120 writing)
| Minutes | Do |
|---|---|
| 0–20 | **Watch** (plan time). First viewing: gist. Second: list elements by category. Underline the **key concept** in the question |
| 20–30 | Write a one-sentence **viewpoint** and a list of 3 interrelationships (e.g. "slow editing + muted music + low light") |
| 30–45 | Intro and **context of production** paragraph (from the context box) |
| 45–60 | **Context of use** paragraph (audience, platform, purpose) |
| 60–110 | 2–3 **analysis paragraphs**, each on one relationship, ending with an **effectiveness judgment** |
| 110–125 | Conclusion with your verdict |
| 125–140 | Proofread |

**Element checklist (to fill while watching):** cinematography (shot size, angle, movement), mise-en-scène (setting, costume, props, colour), editing (pace, transitions), sound (diegetic, non-diegetic, music, silence), lighting, performance, graphics/text, technology used (CGI, animation, AR, motion capture), narrative and genre conventions.

## What each criterion needs (both years)
| Criterion | Evidence in your essay |
|---|---|
| **Explaining: production** | Who made it, why, when, funding, technology, institution (from the context box) |
| **Explaining: use** | Where it was shown, audience, how audiences used it, results (from the context box) |
| **Analysing** | Many accurate elements, **significant interrelationships**, **plausible interpretations** of how they communicate meaning |
| **Appraising** | A clear **viewpoint** answering "how effectively", with **pertinent evidence from the stimulus** |
| **Written literacy** | Organised, cohesive, correct terminology and conventions |

In 2024 the guide's *Explaining* criterion also includes **explaining connections between the key concept and the contexts**: say how the context shaped how the concept (representation, technology, institution) appears.

## 2025

### Q1 Technologies: Mitski, *Love Me More* (music video, 2022)

<Diagram name="sol-film-2025-q1" />
**Question:** How effectively have technologies been used to enhance meaning? Justify by analysing the stimulus and explaining the contexts of production and use, **including the target audience**.

**From the context box:**
- *Production:* Mitski (Japanese-born American singer-songwriter); fourth single from *Laurel Hell* (2022); second Mitski video directed by Christopher Good.
- *Meaning:* the song explores **isolation as a singer-songwriter and longing for connection and validation**. Mitski said a romantic relationship is "the best metaphor, the best narrative vehicle" for telling a story or evoking a feeling.
- *Use:* a music video, circulated online.

**Your viewpoint starter:** *The technologies used in the video are highly effective in enhancing the meaning of isolation and longing, because …*
**Paragraph skeleton:**
1. Context of production: artist, album, director, intent (isolation and validation).
2. Context of use: music video for streaming and online platforms, fans of her indie/alternative music, the target audience (you decide: age, interests, why).
3. Technology 1 + how it works with another element → meaning (isolation/longing).
4. Technology 2 + relationship → meaning (romantic metaphor).
5. Judgment: effectiveness for the target audience, and one limit.

### Q2 Representations: *The Last Job on Earth* (animation, Moth Collective, 2016)

<Diagram name="sol-film-2025-q2" />
**Question:** How effectively have ideas about the future been represented? Analyse the stimulus and explain production and use, including the target audience.

**From the context box:**
- *Production:* animation studio Moth Collective; funded by the **DOEN Foundation** (supports green, socially inclusive and creative innovations).
- *Use:* published alongside the Guardian article "Automation may mean a post-work society but we shouldn't be afraid" in its **sustainable business** section. The Guardian appeals to a range of social classes; **over two-thirds of readers are 35 or older**.
- *Idea:* Paul Mason's quote about the "biggest enigma" of the post-work society: what happens to the self when it cannot define itself against **corporate identity, skill set or seniority**.

**Viewpoint starter:** *The animation represents the future as … effectively/ineffectively because …*
Link every analysis point to **an audience of older, readers of business news** and the **article's message** that a post-work society is not to be feared.

### Q3 Audiences: *The Underdogs* (Apple web film, 2019)

<Diagram name="sol-film-2025-q3" />
**Question:** How effectively does the web film position the audience to engage with the Apple brand?

**From the context box:**
- *Production:* first in a series of web films by Australian director **Mark Molloy**, **commissioned by Apple Business** for the "Apple at Work" campaign; a **B2B (trade) advertisement**; same cast of four office workers in a mini-sitcom; winner of multiple awards.
- *Use:* published on Apple's YouTube channel; the 4th instalment is 8+ minutes and got **2 million views on day one, 78 million views since**.
- *Detail:* the circular pizza box was invented by Apple employees and patented in 2012.

**Viewpoint starter:** *The web film effectively positions the audience … because the sitcom format … while the brand appears …*
Discuss **positioning** (how the audience is encouraged to see products as useful in the workplace), **humour** and **narrative**, and why B2B ads differ from consumer ads.

## 2024

### Q1 Representations: *Silent* (Moonbot Studios in partnership with Dolby, 2014)

<Diagram name="sol-film-2024-q1" />
**Question:** How effectively do the characters represent the evolution of moving images and audio, and the relationship between them? Analyse and appraise the stimulus, considering production and use.

**From the context box:**
- *Production:* animated short by **Moonbot Studios** (Academy Award-winning) with **Dolby**; sound by Academy Award-nominated sound designer **Steve Boeddeker**.
- *Purpose:* represent the **evolution of film technology** and celebrate the **emotional power of audio**. Director Brandon Oldenburg said **Morris Lessmore and The Kid represent picture and sound** respectively.
- *Use:* debuted at the **Scientific and Technical Academy Awards** (15 Feb 2014), so the audience included industry and technical experts. *(Inferred:)* it also promotes Dolby's sound technology.

**Viewpoint starter:** *The characters are an effective/ingenious/limited representation of … because …*
Analyse how each character's **design, movement and sound** work together, and how the relationship between them is shown.

### Q2 Technologies: *Jurassic World: Dominion* (2022), the blind dinosaur scene

<Diagram name="sol-film-2024-q2" />
**Question:** How effectively have technologies been used to **build tension** and **position the audience**?

**From the context box:**
- *Production:* third *Jurassic World* film and sixth in the series (began 1993); director **Colin Trevorrow**; **Universal Pictures**.
- *Scene:* Claire Dearing (Bryce Dallas Howard) is trying to evade a **large, blind dinosaur** after ejecting from a damaged aeroplane into the trees.
- *Use (inferred, not in the context box):* a major franchise release aimed at a broad audience; the clip is available online (the reference list gives a YouTube link).

**Viewpoint starter:** *Technologies such as … build tension effectively by …*
Cover how **sound design, CGI/animatronics, camera and editing** create tension (a blind predator suggests sound and silence), and how the audience is positioned alongside Claire.

### Q3 Institutions: Goldfish Focus Faceoff Lens (Zulu Alpha Kilo, 2022)

<Diagram name="sol-film-2024-q3" />
**Question:** Based on the stimulus, how has the advertising agency created an effective marketing campaign for teenagers?

**From the context box:**
- *Production:* **Campbell's** (Canadian food company) hired the agency **Zulu Alpha Kilo** to market a new Goldfish flavour to **teenagers aged 13–17** using **eye-tracking and facial mesh technology** in a gamified **AR Snapchat lens**, the first to track blinking, tapping, turning and eye movement.
- *Use:* launched Nov 2021 on Snapchat (more than half of Canadian internet users aged 13–25 used it); users shared **high scores** and gameplay earned a **coupon**; on Snapchat for **one month**; the agency **paid to have it promoted** on the camera carousel; success was measured by **playtime and sales**, and the new tomato sauce flavour **quadrupled sales revenue**.

**Viewpoint starter:** *The campaign was highly effective because the institution matched the platform, technology and incentive to its teenage audience, shown by …*
Focus on **institutions** (agency, brand, platform), **audience targeting**, the **gamification** and **rewards**, and how success was measured.

## Writing the viewpoint (the Appraising marks)
| Level in the guide | What yours should do |
|---|---|
| "insightful viewpoint supported by valid and convincing conclusions" | Take a position and qualify it ("highly effective for X, though less so for Y") |
| "pertinent evidence from the stimulus to consistently support conclusions" | After every claim, cite **a specific shot, sound, edit or graphic** |
| "valid conclusions" | End each analysis paragraph with a judgment on effectiveness |
