## Paper structure
| | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|
| Total marks | 66 (10 + 36 + 20) | **67** (10 + 36 + 21) |
| Section 1: multiple choice | 10 | 10 |
| Section 2: short response | 4 questions, 36 marks | 5 questions, 36 marks |
| Section 3: extended response | 1 question, 20 marks (parts a–e) | 1 question, 21 marks (parts a–e) |
| Stimulus book | Yes | Yes |
| Time | 5 min perusal + 120 min | same |

**Pacing:** 120 minutes for 66–67 marks is about **1.8 minutes per mark**. Do the 10 multiple-choice questions in about 15 minutes, leave about 20–25 minutes for the extended response, and don't spend more than about 15 minutes on any 8–10 mark short response.

Calculator: non-programmable scientific. Multiple choice is answered with a 2B pencil in the response book.

## Patterns to revise
1. **Privacy and CIA appear every year**: the 13 **Australian Privacy Principles** and confidentiality/integrity/availability risks with recommendations. Know the APP names, not just ideas.
2. **Diagrams**: a **data flow diagram** appeared in both years (draw one in 2024, interpret one in 2025). Be fluent with external entities, processes, data stores and labelled flows.
3. **Pseudocode and algorithms with JSON**: both years use JSON data and require pseudocode (write or complete). Practise loops, conditions, arithmetic and outputting results.
4. **Data dictionaries and data specifications** (2025): field, data type, format, validation, description.
5. **Encryption**: ciphers (Caesar, Vigenère, Gronsfeld), one-time pad/XOR, RSA/asymmetric encryption, hashing. Both years have a mix of MC and applied questions.
6. **Useability and visual communication**: UI mock-ups, the five useability principles (accessibility, effectiveness, safety, utility, learnability), the elements and principles of visual communication, and evaluating an app against two principles.
7. **Longer questions are multi-part** and scaffolded, with 3–6 mark parts. They start with an *explain* and finish with an *evaluate/recommend*.

## Command terms that show up
**Identify and explain/describe • Analyse the scenario • Use pseudocode to symbolise • Use a data flow diagram to symbolise • Evaluate … against criteria • Recommend • Explain the relationship • Complete • Develop a prototype**.

For a *Recommend* or *Evaluate* answer, name the item, give a reason tied to the scenario, then state the effect. For *Analyse the scenario*, quote details from the stimulus.

## Earlier papers: 2020–2022 (same Unit 4 content, different mark structure)
| Year | Total | Short response | Extended response |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2020 | 72 | 41 (Q11 21, Q12 8, Q13 12) | 21 (Blowfish algorithm, useability principles, evaluation) |
| 2021 | 72 | 36 (DFD 13, CIA/APP/Caesar 18, checksum/encryption 5) | 26 (UI analysis, data exchange components, security recommendations, login redesign) |
| 2022 | 69 | 28 | 31 (DFD 19, wireframe 4, processes 5, impacts 3) |

**2023 (69 marks):** 10 MC (key C A C B A C D B D C) + 34 short response + a 25-mark extended response (no stimulus book). Short response: ticketing system data size and security, with **pseudocode that hashes a password and verifies username and password** (7); a dentist van's personal data security needs and a VPN feature (5); **debugging four errors in a one-time pad cipher** with a fix and justification for each line (9); a smart-plug interface screen and two accessibility features (11). Extended: a school voting system with **XML** for a candidate, features enforcing voting rules, a **14-mark DFD** with a school database for conflicts of interest, and justifying the interrelationships. Debugging pseudocode with justification, and XML, are new to 2023.

**Multiple-choice answers:** 2020 D B B A B A C D D C · 2021 A A A D C B B D B C · 2022 A B C C D D C D D B.

**Recurring every year:** data flow diagrams (2021, 2022, 2024, 2025); Caesar, one-time pad or Blowfish pseudocode and desk checks; confidentiality/integrity/availability risks; Australian Privacy Principles; useability and visual communication; JSON and SQL; hashing, checksums and protocols (HTTP/HTTPS/FTP). From 2024 the marks are spread over more, smaller parts than the single large DFD or algorithm question of 2020–22.

**Guide detail worth knowing**
- 2020 visual communication: *state three principles and describe how each is used in the UI* (e.g. contrast, alignment, repetition, proximity).
- 2021 DFD (13 marks): two external entities, four data stores, five processes, logical flow and syllabus conventions.
- 2021 Q13: recommend AES (128-bit blocks, 128/192/256-bit keys); DES/3DES were described as easily cracked.
- 2022 Q12: assignment, condition, iteration and modularisation defined in one line each.
- 2022 Q15: DFD for a school fundraising app (six processes, three admin-only functions, four data stores).

::: warning Perusal time
Every printed paper from 2020 to 2025 says **15 minutes** perusal, while the 2025 syllabus and the 2026 QCAA timetable say **5 minutes**. This site follows the 2026 timetable; confirm on your exam notice.
:::

## Revision actions
- Run through the **13 APPs** in the [worked examples](/digital-solutions/practice) and match each to a scenario.
- Draw a **context DFD** and **level-1 DFD** for two scenarios from scratch, then check them against 2025 Q11 and 2024 Q14.
- Write pseudocode for: average of an array, count matching items, convert JSON fields, percentage calculation.
- Do both **MC books** in 10 minutes each (the 2025 guide gives answers; use it to check your reasoning).
- Practise "evaluate against two useability principles": choose from the syllabus five (**accessibility, effectiveness, safety, utility, learnability**), apply each to a screen feature, make a verdict.
