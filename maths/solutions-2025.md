### Section 2: short response (42 marks)

#### Q16 (3 marks) Time zones
Nagano is GMT+9 and Santa Cruz is GMT−8, so Santa Cruz is 9 − (−8) = **17 hours behind**.
2:00 pm Monday − 17 h = 2:00 pm − 12 h (2:00 am Monday) − 5 h = **9:00 pm Sunday**.
**Marks:** working (1), time (1), day (1). Do not forget the day change.

#### Q17 (4 marks) Association in a two-way table
a) Year 6: 11 + 6 = **17**. Year 10: 3 + 9 = **12**.
b) Year 6 under 15 min: 11/17 × 100 = **64.7%**. Year 10: 3/12 × 100 = **25%**.
The percentages differ a lot (64.7% is more than double 25%), so **there is an association between year level and travel time**.
**Marks:** both totals (1), each percentage (1 + 1), comparison that justifies the association (1).

#### Q18 (4 marks) Great-circle distance on a parallel
a) (43.5° S, 172.5° E) is **Christchurch**.
b) Same latitude (38.7° S), so use a parallel: angular distance = 178° − 176° = **2°** (Taupo is 176° E, Gisborne 178° E).
Distance = 111.2 × cos(38.7°) × 2 = 111.2 × 0.7804 × 2 = 173.6 ≈ **174 km**.
**Marks:** location (1), angular distance (1), substitution (1), rounded answer (1). The cos(latitude) factor is the trap: you need it for east-west distance but not for north-south.

#### Q19 (4 marks) Seasonal index
a) 2024 average = (7500 + 23 500 + 82 000 + 12 500) ÷ 4 = 125 500 ÷ 4 = **\$31 375**.
b) Q3 ratio 2023 = 94 000 ÷ 32 500 = **2.89**. Q3 ratio 2024 = 82 000 ÷ 31 375 = **2.61**.
Seasonal index = average of the two ratios = (2.89 + 2.61) ÷ 2 = **2.75**.
Meaning: Q3 sales are typically about 2.75 times the quarterly average.

#### Q20 (4 marks) Reducing-balance loan
Monthly rate = 5.4% ÷ 12 = 0.45% = 0.0045.
a) **A(n+1) = 1.0045 × A(n) − 557, A(0) = 24 000**.
b) A(1) = 1.0045 × 24 000 − 557 = 24 108 − 557 = **23 551**.
A(2) = 1.0045 × 23 551 − 557 = **\$23 099.98**.
c) Reduction = 24 000 − 23 099.98 = **\$900.02**.
**Marks:** recurrence (1), working (1), value (1), reduction (1). Keep the unrounded value in your calculator between steps.

#### Q21 (4 marks) Arithmetic or geometric?
a) Differences: 18 − 6 = 12, 30 − 18 = 12, 42 − 30 = 12. A **constant difference of 12**, so it is **arithmetic**. (For geometric the ratios would be equal: 18/6 = 3 but 30/18 = 1.67.)
b) Continue: Day 5 = 54, Day 6 = 66. Plot (1,6), (2,18), (3,30), (4,42), (5,54), (6,66), with day on the horizontal axis.
**Marks (guide):** type of sequence (1), justify with the common difference of 12 (1), first four points plotted (1), days 5 and 6 plotted (1).

#### Q22 (4 marks) Perpetuity
a) A perpetuity pays only the interest each period: payment = \$50 000 × 0.0494 ÷ 26 = **\$95**.
b) $\mathrm{EAR} = (1 + 0.0494/26)^{26} - 1 =$ **5.06% p.a.** (about 0.0506).
Fortnightly means 26 periods a year.
**Marks (guide):** working (1) + payment (1); working (1) + EAR as a percentage (1). Show the formula substitution each time.

#### Q23 (5 marks) Minimum spanning tree
a) P, Q and U joined by three cables form a **cycle** (P–Q–U–P). A **tree is a connected graph with no cycles**, so it is not a tree.
b) Use Kruskal's or Prim's algorithm on the diagram: pick the shortest cables that do not make a cycle until all 6 outlets are connected (5 cables). The guide's MST has cables of **3, 4, 4.2, 6 and 6.5 m**, total **23.7 m**.
**23.7 < 25, so the electrician's claim is reasonable.**
**Marks (guide):** identifies the cycle (1), states a tree has no cycles (1), identifies the MST (1), MST length (1), decision (1).

#### Q24 (5 marks) Least-squares line
a) $R^{2} = 0.997$ → **99.7%** of the variation in earnings is explained by the linear relationship with year.
b) Slope = **49**. Average weekly earnings **increased by \$49 each year**.
c) x = 2035: y = 49 × 2035 − 97 140 = 99 715 − 97 140 = **\$2575**.
**Marks (guide):** (a) percentage (1); (b) slope value (1) + interpretation as an increase per year (1); (c) correct substitution (1) + prediction (1).

#### Q25 (5 marks) Critical path (activities A–G)
a) EST(D) = 12 + 15 = **27 min** (D follows A and B).
b) Critical path A–B–D–F: 12 + 15 + 20 + 8 = **55 min**.
c) LST(E) = 55 − 8 − 14 = **33 min** (work back from the finish through F, then subtract E's duration).
d) Float(G) = LFT(G) − EST(G) − duration(G) = 55 − 28 − 5 = **22 min**.
(Check the activity dependencies on the diagram. The guide's working shows these earliest/latest times.)
**Marks (guide):** one mark each for (a), (b) and (c); (d) working (1) + float (1).

## Paper 2 (38 marks)

#### Q1 (4 marks) Scatterplot
Plot customers (horizontal) against open checkouts (vertical): (6,1), (10,2), (22,5), (18,4), (14,1). Four points rise steadily. **The 4 pm point (14, 1) is an anomaly.** For 14 customers the trend gives about **3** checkouts. 3 − 1 = **2 additional checkouts** should have been open.
**Marks:** axes with suitable scale (1), accurate points (1), reasoning from the trend (1), answer (1).

#### Q2 (5 marks) Geometric depreciation
t(1) = 83 600 and r = 71 060 ÷ 83 600 = **0.85** (check: 60 401 ÷ 71 060 = 0.85).
t(n) = 83 600 × 0.85^(n−1).
End of 2028 → n = 8: $t(8) = 83\,600 \times 0.85^{7} =$ **\$26 800.24**.
One third of the 2021 value = 83 600 ÷ 3 = **\$27 866.67**.
26 800.24 < 27 866.67, so **yes, the 2028 value will be lower than one third of the 2021 value**.
**Marks (guide):** t1 and r (1), geometric rule used (1), 2028 value (1), one-third value (1), decision (1).

#### Q3 (5 marks) Flow network

Pipes (L/s): A→B 32, A→C 38, B→C 12, C→D 20, C→E 24, D→E 20.
Draw the directed network from the table, then test **cuts** (flows from the source side to the river side):
- Cut after A: 32 + 38 = 70
- Cut {A, B}: 38 + 12 = 50
- Cut {A, B, C}: 24 + 20 = 44
- Cut {A, B, C, D}: 24 + 20 = 44

Maximum flow = minimum cut = **44 L/s**. To reach 45 L/s only one pipe can change. Both cuts of 44 contain C→E, so upgrading **C→E from 24 to 25 L/s** makes the cuts 45 (and the rest are above 45).
**Marks:** diagram (1), cuts method (1), original max flow (1), pipe identified (1), new rate **25 L/s** (1).

#### Q4 (5 marks) Shortest closed route

<Diagram name="sol-2025-p2q4-road" />
Draw the weighted graph on S, T, U, V, W with the 10 roads. The shortest closed route that visits all four venues is **S–T–U–W–V–S**: 3 + 4 + 2 + 6 + 10 = **25 km**.
Driving allowance = 25 × \$0.80 = **\$20**.
Machines = 2 + 3 + 4 + 3 = 12 → 12 × \$25 = \$300.
**Total = \$300 + \$20 = \$320.**
(Compare other cycles to be sure it is the minimum, e.g. S–T–W–U–V–S = 28.)

#### Q5 (7 marks) Euler trails

<Diagram name="sol-2025-p2q5-euler" />
Roads: AB 208, BC 105, CD 140, DE 140, EC 208, CA = ?, AE 105. Right angle at B, so use Pythagoras:
$\mathrm{CA}^{2} = 208^{2} + 105^{2} = 54\,289$ → **CA = 233 m**.
Degrees: A 3, B 2, C 4, D 2, E 3. Exactly two odd vertices (A and E), so an **open Eulerian trail** exists from A to E. Usual walk: A–B–C–D–E–C–A–E.
Total = 208 + 105 + 140 + 140 + 208 + 233 + 105 = **1139 m**.
Tuesday: a **closed** Eulerian trail needs all degrees even, so the unusable walkway must be **A–E** (105 m). Route: A–B–C–D–E–C–A, distance **1034 m**.
Speed = 1139 ÷ 17 = **67 m/min**. Time on Tuesday = 1034 ÷ 67 = **15.4 min**.
15.4 > 15, so **she did not finish in under 15 minutes**.
**Marks (guide):** CA distance (1), open Eulerian trail for the usual walk (1), closed Eulerian trail for Tuesday (1), total distance usual walk (1), total distance Tuesday (1), working for time (1), decision (1).

#### Q6 (6 marks) Two sequences meet

<Diagram name="sol-2025-p2q6-hike" />
Fiore: F(n) = 2n. Goran: G(n) = 12 − 3(n − 1) = 15 − 3n (start 15 km from the car park).
Meet when 2n = 15 − 3n → 5n = 15 → **n = 3 hours**, at **6 km** from the car park.
They meet at 10 am + 3 h = 1 pm, break 30 min, resume at **1:30 pm**, arrive at 4 pm, so 2.5 h travelling.
Speed = 6 ÷ 2.5 = **2.4 km/h**.
**Marks (guide):** method to find when and where they meet (1), time since start (1), distance from the car park (1), time the break ends (1), speed (1), logical organisation (1).
A table of values is a good method: n = 0..3 gives F: 0, 2, 4, 6 and G: 15, 12, 9, 6.

#### Q7 (6 marks) Evaluate a prediction
Sum the profits: 2022: 550 000; 2023: 780 000; 2024: 830 000; 2025: 1 200 000.
These rise by about \$200 000 a year on average (a least-squares line through the four sums has slope 200 000). The guide anchors the model at the 2025 value with x = years after 2025:
**y = 200 000x + 1 200 000**.
2040 → x = 15: y = 200 000 × 15 + 1 200 000 = **\$4 200 000**.
4 200 000 > 4 000 000, so **the director's prediction is reasonable**.
(Checking another way: the least-squares line itself gives about \$4.14 million, also above \$4 million.)
**Marks:** sums (1), model (1), substitution (1), prediction (1), decision (1), logical organisation (1).
Evaluate answers should also note a limitation: extrapolating 15 years from only four data points is uncertain.

## How the guide awards marks
The QCAA guide ties every mark to one of the six objectives and lists **one mark per criterion**, so you can see exactly what earns credit. The recurring criteria are:

| Criterion wording in the guide | What you must write |
|---|---|
| "provides mathematical reasoning or working to support the answer" | Show the substitution or method line, not just the answer |
| "calculates / determines …" | The final value, with units and correct rounding |
| "correctly identifies …" | Name the feature (sequence type, path, upgraded pipe) |
| "compares / justifies …" | A sentence linking numbers to the claim |
| "provides decision" | State yes/no, reasonable/not reasonable |
| "shows logical organisation" (6-mark questions) | Order the steps clearly, one idea per line |
| "FT (follow through)" | An earlier error does not cost later marks if your later method is correct |

## What the marks teach you
- **Show the method**: nearly every question gives separate marks for substitution/working before the answer.
- **Finish with a decision** where the question says "justify", "evaluate" or "reasonable". A numerical answer without a sentence loses the last mark.
- **Use the right representation**: Paper 2 asks for a plot, graph or diagram (Q1, Q3, Q4) and marks it.
- **Allow follow-through**: if you make an error early, keep going. The guide awards follow-through marks if later working is correct.
- **Units and rounding**: state km, \$, minutes. Round at the end.
